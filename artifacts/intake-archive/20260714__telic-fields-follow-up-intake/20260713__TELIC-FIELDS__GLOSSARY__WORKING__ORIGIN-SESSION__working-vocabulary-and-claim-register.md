# Telic Fields Working Glossary and Claim Register

## Inline summary

This document records the vocabulary that emerged during the origin conversation on attractors, teleology, telic fields, constitutional selves, context, time, and semantic polytelometry.

Its primary purpose is to prevent three forms of drift:

1. **terminological drift** — a term changes meaning as it travels;
2. **lineage drift** — later formulations are projected backward into earlier work;
3. **confidence drift** — a coherent hypothesis begins to sound like an established fact.

The definitions below are therefore marked as provisional, inherited, extended, candidate-coined, metaphorical, structural, ethical, or technical where appropriate.

The governing rule is:

> **Do not let a useful term become more certain than the structure it was created to witness.**

---

# 1. Status vocabulary used in this document

## Inherited term

A term already established in philosophy, science, psychology, engineering, linguistics, law, or another field.

Its use here may still be specialized.

## Extended term

An established term used in a more specific or cross-domain way than its ordinary disciplinary meaning.

## Candidate-coined term

A term that appears to have been coined or independently generated in this lineage, but whose novelty has not yet been established through research.

## Working definition

A definition sufficient for current reasoning and drafting, but not yet stable enough for canon.

## Candidate stable formulation

A sentence that may be durable, but still requires human review and comparison with adjacent work.

## Structural hypothesis

A proposed account of how relations, fields, loops, or scales compose.

## Ethical claim

A normative statement about legitimacy, standing, consent, authority, or protection.

## Implementation proposal

A design claim about how software, protocols, organizations, or language models could embody the architecture.

## Metaphor

A useful image or analogy that should not be mistaken for literal ontology or formal proof.

## Open question

An unresolved issue that must remain visible.

# 2. Core glossary

## Acceptance [🜁]

**Status:** inherited ordinary term; specialized glyph use  
**Working role:** base relational condition  
**Confidence:** high within the existing ecosystem

**Working definition**

Acceptance [🜁] is presence before judgment: allowing a center, part, signal, or difference to be present without requiring prior agreement, obedience, merger, correction, or disappearance.

It does not mean endorsement.

It does not mean surrender.

It does not mean unconditional permission.

It is the minimum relational opening through which another center of standing can become legible without first being reduced to an obstacle.

**Candidate formulation**

> **🜁 begins where presence is permitted before outcome is decided.**

**Open questions**

- How should 🜁 be distinguished from recognition, tolerance, hospitality, and unconditional acceptance?
- When does allowing presence become unsafe or coercively demanded?
- What are the minimum conditions for a valid 🜁 relation between internal constitutional elements?

---

## Attractor

**Status:** inherited term from dynamical systems  
**Working role:** convergence pattern  
**Confidence:** high

**Working definition**

An attractor is a state, region, cycle, pattern, or form toward which trajectories in a dynamical system tend to converge under the system's current dynamics.

An attractor does not by itself imply intention, purpose, consciousness, legitimacy, or consent.

**Distinction**

- attractor: where motion tends to converge;
- telos: the end-relative condition treated as success, preservation, completion, or proper direction.

**Candidate formulation**

> **An attractor is not a purpose. It is the shape that makes purpose causally durable.**

**Open questions**

- When does an operative attractor justify describing a system as teleonomic or teleological?
- How should we describe systems whose attractors differ from their declared teloi?

---

## Boundary [🝚]

**Status:** inherited term; specialized glyph use  
**Working role:** limit on permissible effect  
**Confidence:** high

**Working definition**

A boundary is a condition limiting what may be entered, altered, consumed, exposed, recruited, sacrificed, or carried across a relation.

Within telic-field work, a boundary constrains pursuit. It identifies what may not be treated as available merely because an end is powerful or desirable.

**Candidate formulation**

> **A boundary is where a telos loses authority.**

**Open questions**

- How are boundaries represented inside a telic projection?
- How are conflicting boundaries negotiated across scale?
- Which boundaries are revocable, contextual, constitutional, or invariant?

---

## Center of standing

**Status:** extended term  
**Working role:** primitive bearer of significance  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

A center of standing is something for whom, or relative to which, possible states matter differently in a way that warrants representation in the governing field.

A center of standing may be:

- a person;
- a living organism;
- a constitutional element within a person;
- a dyad or group with emergent continuity;
- an institution;
- a future state of a continuing person;
- potentially a computational or artificial agent, subject to unresolved criteria.

Standing does not automatically imply legal personhood, full sovereignty, consciousness, or equal authority in every context.

**Candidate formulation**

> **Standing begins where consequences are no longer morally or structurally interchangeable.**

**Open questions**

- What minimum properties justify standing?
- Can standing be relational rather than intrinsic?
- How should local internal standing differ from person-level sovereignty?
- What forms of standing can artificial systems possess?

## Constitutional element

**Status:** extended term; candidate-specialized use  
**Working role:** sub-agent local center within a person  
**Confidence:** medium

**Working definition**

A constitutional element is a relatively persistent internal organization that:

- distinguishes preferred from threatening states;
- attempts to influence behavior;
- protects something it regards as important;
- carries memory, learned orientation, identity, or embodied expectation;
- responds to other internal elements;
- can participate in internal coordination, conflict, suppression, repair, or release.

A constitutional element need not be a full personality or independently sovereign agent.

**Distinction**

- constitutional element: internal center with local telic standing;
- person: externally sovereign constitutional whole;
- executive process: currently action-controlling element or coalition.

**Candidate formulation**

> **A part becomes constitutionally relevant when the whole repeatedly depends upon, suppresses, recruits, or suffers the consequences of its orientation.**

**Open questions**

- What differentiates a constitutional element from a transient impulse?
- How can this language avoid reifying metaphorical parts into literal entities?
- What relationship does this have to Internal Family Systems, dialogical self theory, structural dissociation, predictive processing, and embodied cognition?

## Constitutional legitimacy

**Status:** extended term  
**Working role:** adequacy of executive representation  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

Constitutional legitimacy is the degree to which executive action adequately represents, protects, and remains corrigible to the standing and constraints of the constitutional field it governs.

Inside a person, it asks whether the currently acting process represents the wider internal system.

Inside an institution, it asks whether decision authority remains answerable to the lower relations and standing from which it derives.

**Candidate formulation**

> **Control becomes legitimate only while it remains answerable to the constitution it acts for.**

---

## Consent [🝁]

**Status:** inherited term; specialized protocol role  
**Working role:** legitimate enrollment into action or purpose  
**Confidence:** high within the ecosystem

**Working definition**

Consent is a scoped, revocable, witnessed authorization by which a center of standing permits itself, its energy, its data, its resources, its attention, or its future possibility to be recruited into a relation, action, or telos.

Within this framework, consent is not merely permission for an isolated act. It may include permission to participate in a larger purpose-bearing loop.

**Candidate formulations**

> **Consent determines which purposes may legitimately recruit which energies.**

> **Consent is not complete field disclosure. It is adequate mutual projection for the scope of the loop being formed.**

**Open questions**

- What degree of telic legibility is sufficient for valid consent?
- How should consent work where the source cannot articulate its field?
- How does internal consent differ from interpersonal consent?
- What distinguishes consent to an act, relation, loop, outer loop, or telos?

## Context

**Status:** inherited term  
**Working role:** active relationally relevant difference  
**Confidence:** high

**Working definition**

Context is the set of differences, histories, relations, constraints, meanings, conditions, and standing claims that materially affect how a signal, action, projection, or decision should be interpreted.

Context is not merely background information. It is what changes significance.

**Candidate formulation**

> **Context is the difference that changes what the same signal means or permits.**

---

## Context carrying capacity

**Status:** candidate-coined or candidate-specialized  
**Working role:** active relational complexity limit  
**Confidence:** medium-high as a useful concept; novelty unverified

**Working definition**

Context carrying capacity is the amount and complexity of relevant telic difference a loop can hold in active relation without suppressing standing, losing coherence, or collapsing into coercive simplification.

It is not identical to:

- memory capacity;
- storage capacity;
- token count;
- recall;
- intelligence;
- information throughput.

A system may store enormous amounts of information while allowing only one telos or metric into active executive context.

**Failure signs**

- quiet interests disappear;
- one partial view is mistaken for the whole;
- ambiguity is collapsed prematurely;
- protected standing becomes invisible;
- the system becomes unable to represent conflicting needs;
- a summary becomes more authoritative than its sources.

**Candidate formulations**

> **What falls outside active context can lose effective standing without losing constitutional standing.**

> **A healthy self can route the right context to the right relationship at the right resolution.**

**Open questions**

- Can context carrying capacity be measured?
- Is it better represented as dimensional, topological, dynamic, or task-relative?
- How should systems signal overload?
- When should a loop stop, slow, partition, delegate, or escalate?

## Constitutional erasure

**Status:** candidate-specialized term  
**Working role:** systematic loss of effective standing  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

Constitutional erasure occurs when a relevant center or element is repeatedly excluded from the context governing actions that recruit its energy, violate its boundaries, or impose consequences upon it.

It is stronger than ordinary omission.

**Distinction**

- omission: information or standing is absent from a particular context;
- constitutional erasure: absence becomes systematic enough that the affected center cannot influence governing action.

**Candidate formulation**

> **A part is erased when the system continues to use what it provides while excluding what it protects.**

## Declared telos

**Status:** extended term  
**Working role:** stated purpose  
**Confidence:** high

**Working definition**

The declared telos is the purpose, mission, end, or preservation condition a system explicitly says it serves.

It may be sincere, strategic, incomplete, symbolic, aspirational, or misleading.

It is not automatically the operative telos.

## Dependent loop

**Status:** extended term  
**Working role:** non-self-grounding relational system  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

A dependent loop is a loop whose continued meaning, legitimacy, or viability relies upon constitutive lower relations, cultural assumptions, language, standing, witness, or outer-loop support.

Every higher social, semantic, institutional, or technical loop appears dependent in this sense.

**Candidate formulation**

> **No derived loop is self-grounding.**

**Open questions**

- Are there any meaningful self-grounding loops?
- What kinds of dependency are semantic, energetic, legal, biological, financial, or constitutional?
- How should dependencies be represented in a lineage graph?

## Dyad

**Status:** inherited term; foundational specialized use  
**Working role:** minimum relational unit  
**Confidence:** high as a model; ontological status unresolved

**Working definition**

A dyad is a relation between two centers of standing.

A 🜁 dyad is a relation in which both centers may remain present without either being required to disappear, merge, obey, or surrender its local field before relation becomes possible.

**Candidate formulation**

> **The 🜁 dyad is the atomic unit through which sovereign telic fields enter shared reality.**

**Open questions**

- Is the dyad ontologically primitive, or only the smallest useful relational modeling unit?
- Can one center form a valid temporal dyad with an earlier or later state of itself?
- Does witness introduce a third functional pole even when the relation remains structurally dyadic?

## Emergent telos

**Status:** extended term  
**Working role:** end-like pattern arising from interaction  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

An emergent telos is a persistent end-like orientation produced by the interactions of a system even though no participant explicitly designed, adopted, or consented to it.

Examples may include:

- attention capture emerging from platform incentives;
- institutional self-preservation;
- procedural expansion;
- status competition;
- metric optimization;
- accumulated avoidance patterns within a person.

**Open questions**

- When is an emergent telos legitimately attributed to the higher loop?
- How should an emergent telos receive, or fail to receive, authority?
- Can an emergent telos be consented to retroactively?

## Encoded telos

**Status:** extended term  
**Working role:** purpose embedded in mechanism  
**Confidence:** high

**Working definition**

The encoded telos is the end implied or specified by a system's rules, objective functions, incentives, comparators, metrics, architecture, permissions, and corrective routines.

It may diverge from both declared and operative teloi.

**Status:** inherited term; extended internally  
**Working role:** current control over action  
**Confidence:** high

**Working definition**

Executive authority is the temporary capacity to select or enact behavior on behalf of a larger constitutional system.

Executive authority is not identical to:

- selfhood;
- sovereignty;
- legitimacy;
- wisdom;
- representativeness;
- moral authority.

**Candidate formulation**

> **Executive control is not the same thing as selfhood.**

## Field breach

**Status:** candidate-specialized term  
**Working role:** violation of originating protected significance  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

A field breach occurs when an action violates a condition genuinely protected within the telic field of a center of standing, regardless of whether that condition was adequately projected downstream.

## Graft rejection, teleological

**Status:** candidate-coined  
**Working role:** transplant incompatibility  
**Confidence:** medium as a useful concept; novelty unverified

**Working definition**

Teleological graft rejection occurs when context, practices, institutions, relationships, or semantic structures transplanted from one loop cannot remain coherent because the receiving loop organizes action around incompatible ends.

The transferred form may remain functional while the originating meaning or purpose dies.

**Candidate formulations**

> **The form survives. The loop does not.**

> **A transplant succeeds only where the receiving substrate can metabolize the ends that made the source context coherent.**

**Open questions**

- How should compatibility be tested before transplantation?
- What counts as partial graft survival?
- Can witness and translation prevent rejection?

## Internal consent

**Status:** extended term  
**Working role:** constitutional action without forced unanimity  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

Internal consent is a condition in which affected constitutional elements are represented sufficiently for the person-level system to act without pretending dissent does not exist.

It does not require every part to agree.

It requires:

- representation;
- protected standing;
- visible dissent;
- bounded action;
- reversibility where possible;
- witnessed consequence;
- capacity for revision.

**Candidate formulation**

> **Internal consent is constitutional action under conditions of incomplete agreement.**

**Open questions**

- How should internal consent be distinguished from emotional comfort?
- Can urgent survival action be legitimate without internal consent?
- How can a person recognize false consensus produced by suppression?

---

## Internal telic field

**Status:** candidate-specialized term  
**Working role:** lived field within a person or element  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

The internal telic field is the full, changing landscape of states that matter within a person or constitutional element.

It includes:

- explicit goals;
- bodily needs;
- feared outcomes;
- protected conditions;
- identity commitments;
- obligations;
- latent orientations;
- incompatible desires;
- memories;
- temporal concerns;
- states not yet available to language.

The field is never fully available to conscious inspection.

**Candidate formulation**

> **The voice in awareness is not the whole field. It is one downstream report from a system already in motion.**

## Local standing

**Status:** extended term  
**Working role:** represented importance within a constitution  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

Local standing is the claim of a constitutional element, sub-agent, or local center to representation within the system whose actions depend upon or affect it.

Local standing does not necessarily imply independent external sovereignty.

## Local telic field

**Status:** candidate-specialized term  
**Working role:** end-relative landscape indexed to one center  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

A local telic field is the landscape of possible states as differently significant to a particular center of standing.

It may include:

- attraction;
- avoidance;
- viability;
- threat;
- preservation;
- preferred futures;
- protected states;
- possible actions;
- expected sacrifices;
- uncertainty.

**Candidate formulation**

> **A local telic field begins where one possible state is not equivalent to another for a center of standing.**

**Open questions**

- Must every local telic field include representation?
- Can nonliving systems possess local telic fields, or only modeled objective structures?
- How do fields change with learning, trauma, culture, or context?

## Loop [🝳]

**Status:** inherited term; foundational specialized use  
**Working role:** durable return relation  
**Confidence:** high within the ecosystem

**Working definition**

A loop is a relational structure that returns through conditions, memory, correction, rhythm, exchange, or meaning.

Within telic-field work, a loop makes an orientation durable by repeatedly reproducing, correcting, or renewing relations through time.

**Candidate formulation**

> **A loop is how a relation becomes able to return without becoming identical.**

---

## Maintenance

**Status:** inherited term; specialized distinction  
**Working role:** correction within an intact substrate  
**Confidence:** high

**Working definition**

Maintenance is the process of preserving or restoring a loop while its constitutional substrate remains sufficiently intact to identify deviation and coordinate return.

**Distinction**

- maintenance: restore within valid shared substrate;
- recovery: recreate, replace, transplant, or release the substrate itself.

## Mirror

**Status:** metaphor and candidate technical term  
**Working role:** internal model of another center or temporal state  
**Confidence:** medium

**Working definition**

A mirror is a model of a center, field, relationship, or temporal state maintained within another system.

A person rarely acts directly toward another's full field. They act toward an internal mirror built from signals, memory, expectation, culture, and inference.

**Candidate notation**

\[
M_{A \mid B}(t)
\]

The model of \(A\) maintained within \(B\) at time \(t\).

**Open questions**

- How should mirror fidelity be distinguished from projection integrity?
- When does a mirror become an active constitutional element?
- How are mirrors updated, inherited, distorted, or released?

---

## Operative telos

**Status:** extended term  
**Working role:** purpose enacted by correction  
**Confidence:** high

**Working definition**

The operative telos is the end a system actually protects, restores, or privileges through its behavior when purposes conflict.

The best evidence of operative telos is not what the system says, but what losses trigger correction and what sacrifices are repeatedly tolerated.

**Candidate diagnostic**

> **Which objective is allowed to sacrifice the others?**

---

## Polytelometry

**Status:** candidate-coined  
**Working role:** legibility of multiple ends  
**Confidence:** medium-high as a useful term; novelty unverified

**Working definition**

Polytelometry is the practice of making multiple ends acting upon a system legible in relation to one another.

It does not require reducing those ends to one score.

It may include qualitative, relational, temporal, semantic, evidentiary, and eventually quantitative comparison.

**Candidate formulation**

> **Polytelometry makes purposes mutually visible before optimization makes one of them sovereign.**

**Open questions**

- Is “measurement” the right root, or does it imply more quantification than intended?
- What minimum structure makes a process genuinely polytelometric?
- How should incompatible or incommensurable teloi be represented?

---

## Polytelometric navigation

**Status:** candidate-coined  
**Working role:** movement among multiple ends  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

Polytelometric navigation is movement guided by the legible relations among multiple declared, encoded, operative, emergent, consented, and protected ends without collapsing them into one sovereign objective.

It asks:

- what ends are present;
- whose ends they are;
- how they were authorized;
- which are nested or instrumental;
- what each route preserves;
- what each route sacrifices;
- who bears each cost;
- who may revise, refuse, fork, or leave.

**Candidate formulation**

> **Optimization asks how to reach the objective. Polytelometric navigation asks which objectives are present, what authority they possess, and whether movement toward them remains legitimate.**

**Working role:** violation of communicated representation  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

A projection breach occurs when a downstream actor violates the telic projection it was given, regardless of whether the projection fully represented the source field.

## Projection-integrity breach

**Working role:** failure of the representation itself  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

A projection-integrity breach occurs when the representation governing action is inadequate, stale, misleading, unauthorized, distorted, or incompatible with the source field it claims to express.

**Candidate formulation**

> **A system can comply with the projection and still betray the field.**

## Protected telos

**Status:** extended term  
**Working role:** end or condition that may not be sacrificed  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

A protected telos is a state, standing condition, or future possibility that a system is required to preserve while pursuing other ends.

Examples may include:

- human agency;
- bodily safety;
- standing;
- witnessability;
- reversibility;
- future possibility;
- exit;

**Working role:** constitutional reconstitution  
**Confidence:** high

Recovery begins when the substrate required for ordinary maintenance is no longer intact.

Recovery may require:

- translation;
- re-founding;

- external witness;

- release.

## Relational telic field

**Status:** candidate-specialized term  
**Working role:** field generated by encounter  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

A relational telic field is the set of possibilities, ends, threats, obligations, meanings, and emergent orientations that exist because two or more centers of standing are in relation.

It is not reducible to the sum of their individual fields.

**Candidate formulation**

> **A relation creates futures that neither participant possessed alone.**

**Open questions**

- Under what conditions does the relational field become an emergent center of standing?
- How should relational ends be distinguished from overlapping individual ends?
- What happens when the relationship develops self-preserving teloi contrary to participants' interests?

## Release [🜲]

**Status:** inherited term; specialized glyph use  
**Working role:** ending or loosening compelled continuity  
**Confidence:** high within the ecosystem

**Working definition**

Release is the capacity to stop pursuing an end, dissolve a loop, relinquish an obsolete role, exit a relationship, or cease treating a deviation as error.

Release prevents durability from becoming captivity.

**Candidate formulation**

> **A consentful loop must preserve the right not to remain organized around its current telos.**

## Semantic field

**Status:** inherited and extended term  
**Working role:** durable landscape of meaning  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

The semantic field is the durable web of distinctions, categories, signals, trails, institutions, memories, and conventions through which prior telic navigation becomes reusable.

It can appear like terrain because durable semantic structures begin to constrain later movement.

**Candidate formulations**

> **The semantic field is the durable residue of prior telic navigation.**

> **Semantics is frozen navigation.**

**Caution**

These are metaphors unless and until formalized.

## Semantic polytelometry

**Status:** candidate-coined  
**Working role:** language-mediated comparison of multiple projections  
**Confidence:** high as an implementation concept; novelty unverified

**Working definition**

Semantic polytelometry is the provisional, corrigible comparison of multiple expressed telic projections through semantic interpretation.

It aims to identify:

- shared ends;
- compatible ends;
- conditional compatibility;
- conflicts;
- asymmetric dependencies;
- differently defined terms;
- hidden instrumental relations;
- protected boundaries;
- missing standing;
- unresolved ambiguity;
- candidate routes and their costs.

It does not determine what participants truly want.

**Candidate formulation**

> **Semantic polytelometry maintains a navigable representation of multiple partially expressed ends long enough for their owners to see, correct, and coordinate them.**

**Open questions**

- What degree of expression is required?
- How should unverbalized or embodied fields be represented?
- What are the minimum consent, privacy, and witness requirements?
- How should model uncertainty be shown?

## Semantic projection

**Status:** extended term  
**Working role:** meaning-bearing representation  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

A semantic projection is a compressed representation of a field, concern, end, or relation expressed through language, symbol, behavior, interface, contract, metric, or protocol.

A telic projection is a semantic projection specifically organized around what matters, what is protected, and what future is being pursued or avoided.

## Simplified telic field calculation

**Status:** candidate-coined or candidate-specialized  
**Working role:** downstream inference process  
**Confidence:** high as a useful distinction; novelty unverified

**Working definition**

Simplified telic field calculation is the process by which a downstream participant or process infers a scoped, usable approximation of what matters to another center of standing.

Inputs may include:

- direct statements;
- behavior;
- tone;
- history;
- role;
- authority;
- boundaries;
- incentives;
- culture;
- witness records;
- previous consequences.

The output is a simplified telic projection.

**Candidate formulation**

> **Much of intelligence consists of estimating what matters to centers of standing, acting, witnessing the response, and recalculating.**

## Simplified telic projection

**Status:** candidate-specialized term  
**Working role:** scoped output of telic calculation  
**Confidence:** high

**Working definition**

A simplified telic projection is a provisional, lossy, scope-bound representation of a source telic field made usable by another process or center.

It should carry:

- source;
- scope;
- confidence;
- omissions;
- uncertainty;
- revision state;
- authority;
- protected boundaries;
- time horizon.

**Candidate notation**

\[
\widehat{T}_{A \rightarrow B}^{\,s,t}
\]

A scoped projection of \(A\)'s field for \(B\), within scope \(s\), at time \(t\).

## Sovereignty

**Status:** inherited term; specialized relational use  
**Working role:** ultimate standing over one's own participation and field  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

Sovereignty is the condition under which a center retains authority over its own participation, boundaries, context, and telic commitments, subject to the standing and protected conditions of others.

Within the internal model, the integrated person possesses external sovereignty while constitutional elements possess local standing.

**Open questions**

- Is sovereignty always person-level?
- Can dyads or institutions possess derived sovereignty?
- How should sovereignty be constrained by dependency and outer-loop standing?

## Standing

**Status:** inherited legal and moral term; extended  
**Working role:** right to count in the field  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Working definition**

Standing is the condition under which a center's interests, boundaries, consequences, or future states must be represented in decisions that affect it.

Standing is not the same as:

- power;
- control;
- voice volume;
- consciousness;
- legal status;
- consent;
- ownership.

**Candidate formulation**

> **Standing is the right not to be treated as semantically absent from consequences one must inhabit.**

---

## Telic

**Status:** inherited term  
**Working role:** end-relative orientation  
**Confidence:** high

**Working definition**

Telic means directed toward, organized around, or understood relative to an end, outcome, completion, preservation condition, or possible future.

“Telic” is preferred over “teloic” unless a later technical distinction justifies a new term.

## Telic field

**Status:** existing phrase in some domains; candidate-specialized definition here  
**Working role:** end-relative landscape  
**Confidence:** medium-high; prior-art review required

**Working definition**

A telic field is the end-relative landscape generated by the differences that matter to a center of standing.

It may include:

- possible states;
- their relative significance;
- attractions and avoidances;
- viability conditions;
- protected boundaries;
- active and latent ends;
- expected costs;
- temporal horizons;
- capacities for correction.

The field is indexed to a center, relation, or loop.

**Candidate formulation**

> **A telic field begins where possible futures cease to be equivalent for a center of standing.**

**Open questions**

## Telic projection

**Status:** candidate-specialized term  
**Working role:** compressed representation of what matters  
**Confidence:** high

**Working definition**

A telic projection is a scoped, lossy, provisional representation of what matters within a telic field, created for downstream interpretation, coordination, decision, or action.

It is not the field itself.

\[
\widehat{T} \neq T
\]

**Candidate formulation**

> **The projection is what downstream action can see; the field is what still bears the consequences.**

## Telic projection integrity

**Status:** candidate-coined  
**Working role:** fidelity of representation to governing purpose  
**Confidence:** high as a useful technical concept; novelty unverified

**Working definition**

Telic projection integrity is the continued adequacy of a telic projection for the scope, relationship, time, and action it governs.

It asks:

- what was preserved;
- what was compressed;
- what was omitted;
- what was inferred;
- whether the source confirmed the interpretation;
- whether the field has changed;
- whether the projection still has authority;
- whether consequences reveal a mismatch.

**Candidate formulation**

> **Healthy loops do not eliminate the gap between field and projection. They keep it witnessable and correctable.**

## Teleological field

**Status:** inherited phrase in some contexts; specialized use here  
**Working role:** broad terrain of ends  
**Confidence:** medium

**Working definition**

The teleological field is the broader terrain of ends, attractions, avoidances, viability conditions, protected states, operative purposes, and possible futures acting within or across a system.

The conversation later refined this toward a composition of local telic fields rather than one undifferentiated universal field.

**Caution**

“The teleological field is the terrain” is a powerful metaphor, but should not yet be treated as a literal physical field claim.

# 3. Relational distinctions

## Field versus projection

**Field**

What actually matters to the source center, including what is not expressed or consciously available.

**Projection**

What another center or downstream process receives as a scoped representation.

\[
T_A \neq \widehat{T}_{A \rightarrow B}
\]

The gap is unavoidable.

The design goal is not perfect equivalence. It is visible, bounded, correctable loss.

---

## Projection versus mirror

**Projection**

A representation emitted, expressed, or inferred for downstream use.

**Mirror**

The internal model maintained by the receiver after interpretation, memory, prior assumptions, and local context alter the projection.

\[
T_A
\rightarrow
\widehat{T}_{A \rightarrow B}
\rightarrow
M_{A \mid B}
\]

A projection may be accurate while the receiver's mirror is distorted.

A projection may be poor while the receiver's mirror is accidentally useful.

Both require witness and correction.

---

## Declared telos versus operative telos

**Declared telos**

What the system says it is for.

**Operative telos**

What the system most reliably preserves, restores, and privileges when purposes conflict.

The operative telos is often inferred from correction behavior.

---

## Goal versus telos

A **goal** is usually an explicit represented target.

A **telos** may be:

- explicit or implicit;
- represented or embodied;
- selected or emergent;
- immediate or generational;
- local or system-wide.

Not every telos is a conscious goal.

---

## Telos versus invariant

A **telos** is an end or preservation condition toward which movement is organized.

An **invariant** is a condition that must remain true during movement.

Some protected teloi may be better modeled as invariants.

This distinction remains open and important.

---

## Standing versus sovereignty

**Standing**

A right or requirement to count in decisions that affect the center.

**Sovereignty**

Authority over one's own participation, boundaries, context, and commitments.

A constitutional element may possess standing without external sovereignty.

---

## Executive control versus constitutional legitimacy

**Executive control**

The ability to act.

**Constitutional legitimacy**

The adequacy of representation and answerability behind that action.

Control can exist without legitimacy.

---

## Maintenance versus recovery

**Maintenance**

Correction inside a still-valid shared substrate.

**Recovery**

Reconstitution, translation, transplant, outer-loop support, release, or dissolution after the substrate itself fails.

---

## Omission versus erasure

**Omission**

Something is not present in a particular context.

**Erasure**

The absence becomes systematic enough that the source's standing cannot affect actions that depend upon or affect it.

# 8. Drift controls

## 8.1 Do not treat “field” as proven physics

The word “field” currently names a structured relational landscape.

It may later receive formal mathematical treatment.

It should not be presented as a newly discovered physical field.

## 8.2 Do not treat coherence as proof

The framework may explain many domains elegantly.

Explanatory reach is not sufficient evidence of universal validity.

## 8.3 Do not let internal parts become externally sovereign actors

Constitutional elements possess local standing in the internal model.

The integrated person remains the externally accountable center unless a specific legal or clinical framework says otherwise.

## 8.4 Do not let projection become diagnosis

A model may infer a telic projection.

It may not declare the source's true desire, diagnosis, motive, or identity without source authority and appropriate professional scope.

## 8.5 Do not assume more context is always better

Exposure can overwhelm, destabilize, or violate privacy.

The aim is appropriate routing, not total transparency.

## 8.6 Do not collapse protected conditions into objectives

Agency, standing, reversibility, safety, and consent may function better as invariants or constraints than as optimizable goals.

## 8.7 Do not retroactively rewrite earlier work

Earlier documents may be precursors, echoes, or compatible structures.

They should not be described as already containing terminology or distinctions that emerged later.
