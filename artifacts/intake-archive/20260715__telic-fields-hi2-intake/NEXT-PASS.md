## H.3 — When the System Should Stop

Write the third public reader page.

Develop:

- context capacity is not storage capacity;
- more context is not always better;
- missing standing versus missing data;
- uncertainty and contradiction;
- privacy as a capacity;
- participant burden;
- the cost of delay;
- ask, narrow, pause, escalate, fork, release, and stop;
- the community-relief intake example;
- why fluent systems should become less authoritative as their context fails.

### Public governing lines

> **What falls outside active context can lose effective standing without losing constitutional standing.**

> **When capacity fails, the correct response may be to narrow, delay, delegate, or stop.**

## Shared gate

HI-3 passes only if:

- context capacity remains multidimensional rather than token-count based;
- a system can reduce authority as context fails;
- privacy-preserving omission is recognized as capacity;
- participant burden is represented;
- stop and escalation carry cost-bearer records;
- H.3 explains legitimate stop without presenting paralysis as safety.
