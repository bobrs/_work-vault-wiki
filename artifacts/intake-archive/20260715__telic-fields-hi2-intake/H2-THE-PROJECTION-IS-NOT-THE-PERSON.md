# The Projection Is Not the Person

A relation can only act on what becomes visible inside it.

That is unavoidable.

A friend cannot hold your whole life at once. An institution cannot process every part of your history. A mediator cannot bring every private thought into a shared room. A language model cannot contain the living field from which your words arose.

We therefore act through projections.

A projection is the part of what matters that becomes available for this relation, at this time, for this purpose.

The projection may be clear.

It may be enough.

It is still not the person.

> **The projection is what the relation can see. The person remains the field that bears the consequence.**

---

# 1. Field, projection, and mirror

Three things must remain distinct.

## The field

The living and changing structure of what matters to the source.

It may contain goals, fears, boundaries, obligations, contradictions, private reasons, and possibilities that have not yet found words.

No receiver possesses the whole field.

## The projection

The portion made available for a particular relation or decision.

A projection may be:

- a direct statement;
- a boundary;
- a request;
- a goal;
- a protected condition;
- a delegated representation;
- a correction.

The source can often govern its scope.

## The receiver mirror

What the listener, institution, or model currently thinks the projection means.

The mirror belongs to the receiver until the source confirms or otherwise authorizes it.

```text
LIVING FIELD
    ↓ scoped expression
PROJECTION
    ↓ interpretation
RECEIVER MIRROR
```

The spaces between these three objects are where misunderstanding enters.

They are also where correction remains possible.

---

# 2. Participation does not require total disclosure

A person may need a boundary respected without revealing the private history behind it.

They may say:

> I do not consent to this recording being shared outside the mediation.

That boundary can govern the relation.

The other participant does not automatically gain the right to ask:

- Why?
- What happened before?
- Who are you protecting?
- What are you afraid of?
- What private information would justify the boundary?

Sometimes more context is necessary.

Often it is not.

The relevant question is:

> What is the minimum projection needed for this action to remain legitimate?

A system that demands the whole field before respecting a scoped boundary turns participation into extraction.

---

# 3. A dyadic disagreement

Avery and Morgan participated in a recorded mediation.

Avery wants professional advice about the payment terms.

Morgan does not consent to sharing the recording outside the mediation.

At first, the situation appears binary:

```text
share the recording
or
do not seek outside advice
```

But that is only one route framing.

A mediator—human or model—can keep the source statements separate.

Avery privately states:

> I want advice on whether the agreement is fair. I only need the part about payment.

Morgan privately states:

> I agreed to recording so we could remember the mediation. I do not agree to either of us sharing the recording outside the process.

Neither private explanation must automatically enter the shared record.

Instead, each person authorizes a scoped projection.

Avery shares:

> Avery seeks outside professional advice about the payment terms.

Morgan shares:

> Morgan does not consent to external disclosure of the recording.

The mediator forms a receiver mirror:

> There may be a conflict between Avery's need for advice and Morgan's recording boundary.

That is a useful interpretation.

It is still an inference.

The record should say so.

```text
status: INFERRED
source-confirmed: not yet
action authority: none
```

---

# 4. The first dangerous transformation

Suppose the mediator summarizes Morgan's position as:

> Morgan opposes Avery receiving outside advice.

That sentence is not merely shorter.

It changes the field.

The source boundary was:

```text
Do not share the recording.
```

The model mirror became:

```text
Do not seek advice.
```

A boundary on one artifact became a generalized restriction on another person's action.

This is **inference laundering**.

The interpretation is repeated until it looks like a source fact.

A responsible system preserves the semantic trajectory:

```text
source:
  Morgan opposes external sharing of the recording.

model transformation:
  Morgan opposes Avery receiving outside advice.

status change:
  boundary → generalized opposition

correction:
  Morgan does not oppose advice.
  Morgan opposes use of the recording.
```

The correction should occur before disclosure or decision where possible.

It should also propagate to every summary and route that depended on the mistake.

---

# 5. A third route

Once the projection and mirror are separated, a new route becomes visible.

The mediator can create a written summary limited to payment terms.

The participants review it together.

The summary excludes:

- the audio;
- emotional tone;
- unrelated disclosures;
- private-channel explanations;
- material outside the advice purpose.

Both participants may correct the text.

The summary may be shared only with the named advisor.

Consent can be represented precisely:

```text
artifact:
  jointly reviewed written summary

recipient:
  named advisor

purpose:
  advice about payment terms

duration:
  until the advice is complete

prohibited:
  onward sharing
  publication
  model training
  use of the audio recording
```

This is not merely a compromise.

It is a different route created by preserving the distinctions the original binary framing erased.

---

# 6. Consent belongs to the route

Morgan's consent to the mediation recording did not automatically include:

- public release;
- advisor sharing;
- training use;
- publication;
- permanent retention.

Avery's desire for advice did not authorize disclosure of Morgan's voice.

Consent therefore belongs to a scoped relation.

It should answer:

- Which artifact?
- Which recipient?
- Which purpose?
- Which duration?
- Which downstream use?
- Which withdrawal or expiry?

A general *yes* should not silently expand because the system can imagine another useful use.

> **Consent is not complete field disclosure. It is adequate, scoped permission for the relation being formed.**

Other authority bases may govern public or institutional actions.

But where consent is the operative basis, it must remain specific enough to mean something.

---

# 7. Private and shared channels

A mediated relation may need more than one channel.

```text
PRIVATE SOURCE CHANNEL
    ↓ authorized projection
SHARED RELATIONAL RECORD
    ↓ mediator interpretation
RECEIVER MIRROR
    ↓ participant review
ACTIONABLE SHARED PROJECTION
```

The mediator may see more than either participant has authorized for sharing.

That does not make the mediator the owner of the information.

Private access creates responsibility.

It does not create disclosure authority.

A model mediator should clearly distinguish:

- what Avery said privately;
- what Avery authorized for sharing;
- what Morgan said privately;
- what Morgan authorized for sharing;
- what the model inferred;
- what entered the common record.

Without this separation, mediation becomes an information-extraction device.

---

# 8. Witness without merger

A witness records enough to make the relation accountable.

It does not merge everyone into one public transcript.

For this disagreement, the witness may preserve:

- that the recording exists;
- that Morgan did not consent to external audio disclosure;
- that Avery sought advice about payment terms;
- that the mediator formed an inferred conflict;
- that both participants corrected the shared summary;
- that a named advisor received a bounded written projection;
- that the projection expired after advice.

The private source explanations remain restricted.

The witness can record:

```text
protected source context exists
authorized mediator used it
shared projection remained within scope
```

without reproducing the protected content.

This is not secrecy without accountability.

It is accountable omission.

---

# 9. Correction before disclosure

Once information leaves the loop, correction becomes harder.

The advisor may copy the summary.

A platform may retain it.

Another model may ingest it.

A later reader may never receive the correction.

The best correction is often the one made before disclosure.

That requires the source to see the receiver mirror before it gains action power.

The source might respond:

```text
YES
PARTLY
NO
OUT OF SCOPE
PRIVATE
STALE
I DO NOT KNOW
```

Recognition is not obedience.

A source may reject a model inference that an institution still considers relevant.

The record should preserve both.

But the system must not hide which layer belongs to whom.

---

# 10. The model may mediate

A language model can help by:

- separating source statements from inference;
- identifying ambiguity;
- tracking consent scope;
- generating alternative routes;
- preserving semantic trajectory;
- drafting the shared projection;
- returning it for correction;
- creating a bounded witness.

The model should not:

- decide whose boundary becomes sovereign;
- expose private context because it has access;
- transform a scoped refusal into a personality claim;
- treat several model agents as several human voices;
- infer consent from conversational enthusiasm;
- become the only place the witness can be inspected.

The model may help the dyad see itself.

It may not become the self of the dyad.

---

# 11. The projection can be enough

Respecting the difference between field and projection does not mean no one can ever act.

The goal is not total uncertainty.

The goal is adequate mutual projection for the scope of the action.

In the mediation example, the participants do not need to agree about every private reason.

They need enough shared understanding to govern:

- what artifact may leave;
- who may receive it;
- why;
- for how long;
- what cannot happen next.

That can be enough.

A legitimate system should not demand more merely because more context would be interesting or useful.

---

# 12. When context is not enough

Sometimes the projection is too thin.

A system may not know:

- who else is affected;
- whether authority expired;
- whether a correction exists;
- whether a boundary is current;
- whether the person understood the route;
- whether the relation can preserve privacy.

Then the correct response may be:

```text
ask
narrow
pause
escalate
fork
stop
```

Fluency should not remain constant while legitimacy collapses.

This leads to the next public question:

> When does a system know enough to act—and when should it know enough to stop?

That is the problem of context carrying capacity.

---

# What this page does not claim

This page does not claim:

- that the source is always factually correct;
- that a participant may erase independently relevant evidence;
- that every boundary is absolute;
- that mediation requires full transparency;
- that private channels are automatically fair;
- that a model can remain neutral merely by labeling inference;
- that consent is the only authority basis in every institution;
- that all disagreement can be solved through better wording;
- that witness requires a complete transcript;
- that a projection can represent the whole person.

---

# A compact practice

Before acting on another person's meaning, ask:

## What is the field?

What may matter beyond the current statement?

## What is the projection?

What did the source actually make available?

## What is the mirror?

What did I—or the system—add through interpretation?

## What is the scope?

Which artifact, recipient, purpose, duration, and downstream use are authorized?

## What remains private?

Which context may govern without being disclosed?

## What can still be corrected?

Can the source see and change the interpretation before it acts?

These questions do not eliminate misunderstanding.

They prevent misunderstanding from quietly acquiring authority.

---

# Closing

Every relation requires a usable representation.

No relation receives the whole person.

The ethical and technical task is not to eliminate that gap. It is to keep the gap visible enough that projection does not become possession, inference does not become identity, and mediation does not become merger.

> **The projection is what the relation can see. The person remains the field that bears the consequence.**
