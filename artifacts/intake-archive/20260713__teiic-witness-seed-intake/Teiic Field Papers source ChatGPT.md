What the wiki requires
The governing rules are:

Witness before mutation. Source material should be inventoried before renaming, moving, merging, rewriting, or canonizing it.

The source artifact and wiki page have different jobs. The artifact is the record; the wiki page is its navigational, interpretive, and relational interface.

Markdown is the preferred working form, while original inputs remain preserved as evidence.

Expand existing pages before creating unnecessary branches, but create a branch when a durable seam has genuinely appeared.

Standard naming is not canonization. A normalized filename only establishes a stable source identity; conceptual canon requires separate human review.

Do not silently promote material to canon. New work should begin as draft or candidate unless explicitly approved. 

Mature wiki pages should preserve the core claim, key ideas, important formulations, implied model, connections, tensions, and open questions.

The wiki should eventually show what changed in the larger map because an artifact exists, not merely list its filename.
