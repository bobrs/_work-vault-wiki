# ULiUA Micro‑Aphorisms

## Core Aphorism (ULiUA)

If you don’t know what you want to do, notice what you’re doing.  
If you want to do it, you know.  
If you don’t, you also know.


---

## Variants & Adjacent Forms

### Softer / Lullaby
If you don’t know what you want to do, just notice what you’re doing.  
Wanting tells you.  
Not wanting tells you too.

### Poetic / Reflective
When you don’t know what you want to do, notice what you’re doing.  
Desire is information.  
So is its absence.

### Ultra‑Minimal
Unsure what to do?  
Notice what you’re doing.  
Wanting knows.

---

## Expanded Explanation (For the Reasonable Mind)

When people say they "don’t know what they want," the confusion usually isn’t about lack of information—it’s about **divided consent**.

At any moment, you are already doing *something*. That action contains information.

1. **Attention precedes clarity**  
   Instead of forcing a decision, simply notice what you are currently doing. This removes pressure and creates a neutral observation point.

2. **Desire is a signal, not a command**  
   If you *want* to be doing the thing you’re doing, that wanting is real data. You don’t need further justification. Wanting is sufficient.

3. **Resistance is also information**  
   If you do *not* want to be doing what you’re doing, that too is clear knowledge—even if you don’t yet know the alternative.

4. **Knowing does not require options**  
   You don’t need to know what you want *instead* in order to know whether you consent to what is happening now.

This reframes indecision as a **present‑moment consent check**, not a future‑oriented optimization problem.

The result is gentle clarity without self‑coercion.

## Conceptual Notes (Internal)

- This aphorism encodes **Consent Loop‑0**: awareness → felt signal → self‑authorization.
- No imperative force; replaces command with attention.
- Preserves sovereignty by validating *both* wanting and not‑wanting as complete information.
- Embodied knowing precedes cognitive justification.
