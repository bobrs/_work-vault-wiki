# Orientation Summary: What I Am Seeking

## Technical Name

**Steerability Engineering for Asymmetric Visibility Systems**

(Alternate working labels: "Combinatorial Drift Mapping," "Inference Boundary Governance," "Reversibility-First Systems Design.")

---

## Gentle Expansion

I am interested in understanding and mapping how capability expansion, inference drift, reversibility constraints, and incentive gradients interact in complex sociotechnical systems. The goal is not moral enforcement or prohibition, but increased legibility and steerability.

Specifically:

- Making scope expansion visible across subsystem boundaries.
- Detecting when inference power expands without explicit declaration.
- Preserving or increasing reversibility as systems scale.
- Observing how short-term optimization pressures distort long-horizon stability.
- Reducing asymmetric invisibility so that power gradients are declared rather than hidden.

This work treats systems as navigable environments rather than moral battlegrounds. The aim is to improve instrumentation, not to eliminate predation, competition, or incentive dynamics—but to ensure they operate on a stable, legible substrate.

---

## Who I Am Looking For

I am looking for peers who:

- Think in feedback loops and attractors rather than binaries.
- Care about steerability, not purity.
- Are comfortable with complexity and combinatorial state spaces.
- Have experience in AI governance, cybersecurity architecture, systems theory, cybernetics, institutional economics, mechanism design, or related domains.
- Value reversibility and scope discipline as structural virtues.
- Are more interested in modeling failure modes than assigning blame.
- Enjoy diagramming spirals, thresholds, and phase transitions for their own sake.

These are likely:

- Mid-career technologists who have seen drift firsthand.
- Researchers in systems science or AI governance.
- Security architects frustrated with compliance theater.
- Builders who want better instrumentation rather than louder regulation.

The goal is not audience. The goal is peer dialogue around structural patterns of drift, asymmetry, and alignment.
