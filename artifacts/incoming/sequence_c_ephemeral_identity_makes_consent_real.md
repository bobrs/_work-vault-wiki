# Sequence C — Ephemeral Identity Makes Consent Real

**Purpose**  
This sequence names the missing prerequisite that makes all prior claims operational:

> *Without ephemeral identity, consent is not real.*

It reframes identity from a static attribute into a **situated mechanism** that enables valid consent, revocation, and trust.

---

## The Spine (what never changes)

- Consent requires the ability to enter *and* exit.
- Persistent identity removes exit.
- No exit → coercion (even if polite).
- Ephemeral identity is not anonymity; it is scoped continuity.

---

## Post 1 — Consent Requires Exit

**Core claim**  
Consent without the ability to exit is not consent.

**Observation**  
Valid consent requires:
- time bounds
- scope limits
- revocation
- exit without penalty

If exit is costly or impossible, consent collapses into compliance.

**Compressed line**  
> “If you can’t leave, you didn’t consent.”

---

## Post 2 — Persistent Identity Removes Exit

**Core claim**  
Persistent identity quietly undermines consent.

**Observation**  
When identity is:
- global
- permanent
- cross‑contextual

then choices follow you.

Past participation becomes future leverage.

**Failure mode**  
People comply to avoid consequences—not because they agree.

**Compressed line**  
> “Persistent identity turns consent into compliance.”

---

## Post 3 — Ephemeral Identity ≠ Anonymity

**Core claim**  
Ephemeral identity is often misunderstood.

**Clarification**  
Ephemeral identity is:
- contextual
- bounded
- accountable *within scope*
- dissolvable by default

It is not:
- anonymity
- lack of responsibility
- evasion

**Key point**  
Accountability can exist *inside* a context without permanent capture.

**Compressed line**  
> “Ephemeral identity is accountability without permanent capture.”

---

## Post 4 — Identity Bridges Must Be Consented

**Core claim**  
Continuity across contexts must be explicit.

**Observation**  
Moving from one context to another requires a bridge:
- linking identities
- carrying reputation
- transferring meaning

Without consented bridges, identity becomes surveillance.

**Implication**  
Linkability should be opt‑in, not assumed.

**Compressed line**  
> “Identity continuity should be granted, not assumed.”

---

## Post 5 — Ephemeral Identity Enables Trust at Scale

**Core claim**  
Trust scales only when consent remains real.

**Observation**  
Ephemeral identity allows:
- safe participation
- honest experimentation
- early boundary testing

Because mistakes don’t permanently brand the participant.

**Result**  
People engage more fully—and systems learn faster *without coercion*.

**Compressed line**  
> “Ephemeral identity makes trust scalable.”
