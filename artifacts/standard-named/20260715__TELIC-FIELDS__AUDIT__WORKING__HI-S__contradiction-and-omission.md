# Qualified tensions

## 1. Consent versus non-consent authority

Potential conflict:

Some public formulations center consent, while technical records allow lawful public mandate, fiduciary duty, contract, license, or emergency authority.

Resolution:

Use:

> consent where applicable, or another visible and reviewable authority basis

Never record another basis as consent.

## 2. Standing versus veto

Potential conflict:

Public emphasis on affected standing can be read as universal blocking power.

Resolution:

Standing requires admission, answerability, and consequence representation.

Veto requires a separately authorized protected condition or governance rule.

## 3. Protected condition versus absolute claim

Potential conflict:

A protected condition can become an unreviewable rhetorical trump.

Resolution:

Every protected condition requires:

```text
source
protected centers
scope
authority
evidence
override process
review
status
```

## 4. Witness versus privacy

Potential conflict:

Witnessability can sound like total transparency.

Resolution:

Witness is bounded and role-scoped.

Protected omission and selective disclosure remain conforming when the gate result can be verified without exposing private content.

## 5. Correction versus historical truth

Potential conflict:

A corrected record may appear to erase the fact that a prior record governed.

Resolution:

Correction updates active governance and preserves the prior version, action history, and descendant impact.

## 6. Withdrawal versus unlearning

Potential conflict:

Public withdrawal language may imply exact model forgetting.

Resolution:

Separate governance actions from technical capability.

Use evidence-qualified labels such as:

```text
source deleted
runtime retrieval blocked
future training blocked
approximate unlearning performed
complete removal not established
```

## 7. Human re-entry versus human supremacy

Potential conflict:

Escalation to humans may imply that human judgment is always legitimate or technically superior.

Resolution:

Human re-entry is required when protected human stakes cannot remain safe, reversible, or answerable inside the current loop.

The human still requires standing, authority, context, and witness.

## 8. Conformance versus legitimacy

Potential conflict:

A system may use conformance as a certification of justice or safety.

Resolution:

Conformance verifies implementation of a declared profile.

It does not certify legal, ethical, clinical, democratic, scientific, or safety legitimacy.
