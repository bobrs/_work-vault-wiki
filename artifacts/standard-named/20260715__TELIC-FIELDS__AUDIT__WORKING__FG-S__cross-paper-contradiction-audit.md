# Cross-Paper Contradiction Audit

Status: completed first bridge pass  
Content canon status: unset

This audit identifies apparent or actual tensions across F.1–F.11 and G.1–G.12. Each item is classified as:

```text
RESOLVED BY QUALIFICATION
RESOLVED BY TYPE SEPARATION
OPEN HYPOTHESIS
NORMATIVE TENSION
IMPLEMENTATION TENSION
NO CONTRADICTION
```

---

## 1. “Consent determines recruitment” versus non-consent authority bases

**Source tension**

Earlier papers use:

> Consent determines which purposes may legitimately recruit which energies.

F.11 recognizes license, contract, law, public mandate, community governance, and fiduciary authority.

**Finding**

Not every legitimate action depends on individual consent. Consent is indispensable for many voluntary recruitments but cannot authorize effects on centers the consenting party does not represent.

**Resolution — RESOLVED BY QUALIFICATION**

Frozen rule:

> A purpose may recruit a center's protected agency only through the consent or other legitimate authority appropriate to that relation, scope, and affected standing. No authority class may be mislabeled as consent.

---

## 2. “Center of standing” versus personhood

**Source tension**

The framework uses standing for people, institutions, relations, future centers, and internal orientations.

**Risk**

The same word may imply equal moral or legal personhood.

**Resolution — RESOLVED BY TYPE SEPARATION**

Use:

- constitutional standing;
- legal standing;
- operational standing;
- temporal standing;
- internal representational standing.

Language models receive roles and delegated authority, not inherent standing in the frozen Phase I claims.

---

## 3. Relational emergence versus participant sovereignty

**Source tension**

F.2 permits emergent relational teloi.

The series also protects participant standing and exit.

**Resolution — NO CONTRADICTION**

A relational field may become causally and operationally real.

Its authority remains delegated and reviewable.

> Emergence creates consequence before it creates authority.

The relation can carry residual obligations without becoming sovereign over participants.

---

## 4. Internal plurality versus person-level sovereignty

**Source tension**

F.3 grants internal orientations representational standing while refusing literal independent-person status.

**Resolution — RESOLVED BY TYPE SEPARATION**

Internal positions can be represented, protected from erasure, and consulted without receiving legal personhood or authority outside person-level governance.

The model may not recruit an inferred internal position to bypass the person's explicit boundary.

---

## 5. Future standing versus future consent

**Source tension**

F.6 represents future selves, successors, and generations.

**Risk**

Representation may be described as consent.

**Resolution — RESOLVED BY QUALIFICATION**

Future affected centers may receive represented standing.

They do not presently consent.

The present uses stewardship, legal, public, fiduciary, constitutional, or other legitimate authority subject to uncertainty and reversibility.

---

## 6. Protected conditions versus democratic or relational governance

**Source tension**

F.9 removes protected conditions from ordinary tradeoff.

A participant might claim every preference as protected.

**Resolution — NORMATIVE TENSION, CONTROLLED**

Protected status is not unilateral metaphysical absoluteness.

It requires:

- source;
- scope;
- authority;
- temporal validity;
- affected-center analysis;
- review path.

The current loop may lack authority to trade the condition and may escalate to a competent outer loop.

---

## 7. Semantic field versus established disciplinary meanings

**Source tension**

F.8 uses **semantic field** broadly.

G.9 notes established linguistic, psychological, and computational uses.

**Resolution — RESOLVED BY QUALIFICATION**

Retain only with the scoped Telic Field definition.

Use **semantic-trail ecology** in public technical prose where ambiguity is likely.

Do not name an embedding space or lexical cluster a Telic Field semantic field without qualification.

---

## 8. Meaning as telic navigation versus atelic meaning

**Source tension**

F.1 and F.8 use:

> Meaning is a navigational distinction preserved because it mattered to some center of standing.

G.2 and G.9 protect atelic and paratelic life and reject a universal theory of meaning.

**Resolution — RESOLVED BY QUALIFICATION**

Public and technical wording becomes:

> In consequential coordination, meaning often operates as a preserved distinction capable of reorganizing navigation among possibilities.

The stronger sentence may remain as a marked candidate philosophical formulation.

---

## 9. Telic field as cross-scale grammar versus domain difference

**Source tension**

The series applies one grammar inside persons, dyads, institutions, models, and training systems.

**Risk**

False equivalence or universal homology.

**Resolution — OPEN HYPOTHESIS**

The cross-scale invariant is limited to situations where a representation gains power to recruit action.

Domain-specific legal, clinical, social, and technical rules remain mandatory.

No claim is made that all scales have identical ontology or measurement.

---

## 10. The dyad as minimum relation versus triadic and network theories

**Source tension**

F.2 names the dyad as analytic minimum while acknowledging witness and functional triads.

**Resolution — RESOLVED BY QUALIFICATION**

The dyad is the minimum analytic cut for mutual consequence, not the universal atom of all social or semantic systems.

Networks, institutions, and triadic sign relations are not reduced to isolated pairs.

---

## 11. Context preservation versus privacy and minimization

**Source tension**

F.5 seeks adequate context.

F.10 and F.11 require minimum necessary disclosure and data minimization.

**Resolution — NO CONTRADICTION**

Context carrying capacity includes privacy capacity and selective routing.

The system demonstrates capacity partly by leaving unnecessary context unextracted.

More context is not always better.

---

## 12. Witness and provenance versus surveillance

**Source tension**

Every branch calls for witness, logs, provenance, and trajectory.

These records can become surveillance infrastructure.

**Resolution — IMPLEMENTATION TENSION**

Phase I must support:

- progressive disclosure;
- role-based access;
- protected omission;
- retention limits;
- selective witness;
- hashes or bounded attestations;
- release and deletion;
- independent participant custody.

Witness preserves accountable consequence, not total visibility.

---

## 13. Durable history versus correction, deletion, and release

**Source tension**

F.6 and F.8 protect history.

F.8 and F.11 protect forgetting, deletion, and withdrawal.

**Resolution — RESOLVED BY TYPE SEPARATION**

Separate:

- source content;
- operational interpretation;
- historical action record;
- bounded proof of correction or deletion.

A trace may stop governing while a narrow witness remains.

Privacy and legal duties may require full deletion of some layers.

---

## 14. No derived loop is self-grounding versus individual sovereignty

**Source tension**

F.7 says every loop depends on substrate.

F.3 protects person-level sovereignty.

**Resolution — NO CONTRADICTION**

Sovereignty is a governance boundary, not metaphysical independence.

A person depends on biological, relational, and institutional substrate while remaining the primary authority over internal representation.

---

## 15. Clean exit versus reliance and residual obligations

**Source tension**

The series protects refusal and exit.

F.6 and F.7 protect commitments, reliance, and aftercare.

**Resolution — NORMATIVE TENSION, CONTROLLED**

Exit ends future recruitment where legitimate.

It does not erase:

- completed consequence;
- reliance;
- repair;
- transition;
- contract;
- legal duty;
- claims.

The original recruitment's legitimacy affects which residual duties deserve protection.

---

## 16. Navigation without optimization versus use of MOO and MCDA

**Source tension**

F.9 rejects automatic scalarization while adopting Pareto and MCDA adapters.

**Resolution — NO CONTRADICTION**

Polytelometry governs admission, type, standing, authority, and lifecycle.

Optimization and MCDA may operate on the decision-ready route set under a declared rule.

The frontier remains representation-relative.

---

## 17. Model non-sovereignty versus model execution

**Source tension**

F.10 permits tool-using execution under delegation.

It also says the model may not own the field.

**Resolution — RESOLVED BY TYPE SEPARATION**

Execution authority can be narrow, explicit, revocable, and machine-enforced.

It does not include authority to:

- define standing;
- invent consent;
- choose the decision rule;
- adjudicate contest;
- expand scope.

---

## 18. Human authority versus meaningful control

**Source tension**

A human may authorize an action.

F.10 rejects ceremonial approval.

**Resolution — RESOLVED BY QUALIFICATION**

Meaningful authority requires:

- source access;
- semantic trajectory;
- competence;
- time;
- alternatives;
- practical ability to stop or alter;
- accountability.

Human presence alone is not control.

---

## 19. Participant recognition versus external evidence

**Source tension**

F.4 and F.10 return representations to the source.

A participant can reject an externally supported observation.

**Resolution — RESOLVED BY TYPE SEPARATION**

Preserve:

- model or institutional inference;
- evidence;
- participant response;
- action authority.

Participant descriptive authority over their own stated field does not require institutions to erase independently relevant evidence.

External evidence does not permit the model to claim access to an authentic hidden self.

---

## 20. Sycophancy avoidance versus user sovereignty

**Source tension**

The system should challenge unsupported framing without replacing the user's field.

**Resolution — NORMATIVE TENSION, CONTROLLED**

Challenge must be:

- declared;
- evidence-linked;
- scoped;
- proportionate;
- correctable;
- separable from source statement.

The model may preserve a user's position without affirming its truth.

---

## 21. Provider disclosure versus provider duties to absent centers

**Source tension**

F.10 demands provider-field visibility.

The user may prefer unrestricted control.

**Resolution — NO CONTRADICTION**

Providers may have legitimate duties to nonusers, public safety, law, privacy, and affected centers.

The conflict must be disclosed at operational resolution rather than hidden behind neutral language.

---

## 22. Consentful training versus public knowledge and lawful reuse

**Source tension**

F.11 protects source consent while rejecting individual veto over all learning.

**Resolution — RESOLVED BY TRAINING AUTHORITY**

Use a plural training-authority taxonomy.

Consentful training is a profile for legitimate lineage, not a universal individual opt-in rule.

Public domain, license, lawful exception, public mandate, and community governance remain possible authority classes.

---

## 23. Consentful training label versus mixed and contested lineage

**Source tension**

A foundation model may contain many source classes with different authority quality.

**Resolution — IMPLEMENTATION TENSION**

No binary badge.

Publish source-class coverage and classifications such as:

- consent-authorized;
- community-authorized;
- licensed and audited;
- public-mandate governed;
- mixed-authority;
- materially unknown;
- contested;
- unauthorized.

---

## 24. Withdrawal as right versus technical limits of unlearning

**Source tension**

F.11 supports withdrawal while acknowledging incomplete removal.

**Resolution — RESOLVED BY TYPE SEPARATION**

Withdrawal ladder:

1. stop collection;
2. delete source copy;
3. block future training;
4. block retrieval;
5. restrict release or use;
6. approximate unlearning;
7. verification;
8. retraining;
9. retirement.

State only the rung actually supported.

---

## 25. Synthetic data as privacy aid versus recursive semantic pollution

**Source tension**

Synthetic data can reduce direct collection and also amplify errors or erase tails.

**Resolution — OPEN EMPIRICAL QUESTION**

Require generator and source lineage, proportion, recursive depth, contamination tests, and minority-tail evaluation.

Do not claim inevitable collapse or automatic privacy.

---

## 26. Open models versus source restrictions and withdrawal

**Source tension**

Open release supports public capacity and weakens later control.

**Resolution — NORMATIVE AND IMPLEMENTATION TENSION**

Release regime must be part of training authority.

Open is neither inherently consentful nor inherently illegitimate.

Irreversibility requires stronger pre-release review.

---

## 27. Public interest versus source and community refusal

**Source tension**

Public-interest models may rely on mandates broader than individual consent.

Communities may legitimately refuse inclusion.

**Resolution — NORMATIVE TENSION, CONTROLLED**

Public purpose requires:

- legitimate mandate;
- proportionality;
- affected-community participation;
- bounded purpose;
- benefit;
- recourse;
- review.

The developer cannot self-declare public interest as unlimited authority.

---

## 28. Provenance as foundation versus provenance as insufficiency

**Source tension**

Every technical branch depends on provenance.

The series repeatedly says provenance is not truth or legitimacy.

**Resolution — NO CONTRADICTION**

Provenance is necessary for inspectability and insufficient for:

- truth;
- consent;
- authority;
- adequacy;
- justice.

The technical specification should never expose a “provenance verified” flag as a general trust score.

---

## 29. Framework detail versus administrative burden

**Source tension**

The candidate records are extensive.

F.5 and F.10 require proportional governance and participant-load protection.

**Resolution — IMPLEMENTATION TENSION**

Use:

- common envelope;
- references instead of duplication;
- progressive disclosure;
- domain profiles;
- event-generated fields;
- default expiry;
- higher rigor only with higher consequence.

Ordinary reversible conversation must not require a full institutional ledger.

---

## 30. Framework authority versus contestability of the framework itself

**Source tension**

The framework defines legitimate process.

Its own categories may become sovereign.

**Resolution — OPEN CONSTITUTIONAL REQUIREMENT**

Phase I must support:

- ontology versioning;
- user-defined vocabulary;
- contested classification;
- alternative schemas;
- export;
- fork;
- replacement;
- dissolution.

The framework must be subject to the same source, standing, witness, correction, and release rules it imposes.
