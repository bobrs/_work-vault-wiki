# HI-7 Gate Review

Status: pass with conditions  
Content canon status: unset

## Gate question

Can the Telic Field architecture support public and institutional decision without:

- inventing one collective mind;
- counting before standing is assembled;
- hiding agenda and option construction;
- treating protected conditions as compensable preferences;
- treating weights or methods as neutral;
- converting majority, consensus, or abstention into authority they do not carry;
- erasing dissent after selection;
- hiding the cost of delay;
- allowing implementation consequence to disappear from public review?

## Overall result

# PASS WITH CONDITIONS

I.7 and H.7 may advance.

No frozen term requires retirement.

`Public-field assembly`, `standing admission`, `option-set witness`, `protected condition`, `decision-rule portfolio`, `route portfolio`, `dissent trail`, `delay bearer`, and `consequence return` remain viable candidate governance terms.

---

# 1. Option-set expansion

## Result

Pass.

The initial administrative frame offered:

- regional-hospital redirection;
- immediate renovation;
- commercial lease.

Public challenge exposed three additional routes:

- mobile clinic;
- local-practice partnership;
- mobile service plus renovation review.

The combined route remained labeled as model-assisted and human-reviewed.

## Finding

An omitted route is not a rejected route.

Option authorship and exclusion are part of the constitutional record.

---

# 2. Minority standing

## Result

Pass.

Disabled patients entered through direct consequence and rights-holder standing.

Their protected-condition claim required every route to answer minimum accessible service.

They did not receive automatic authority to choose the final route.

## Finding

Minority standing changes what the decision must answer.

It does not automatically govern every answer.

---

# 3. Protected condition

## Result

Pass.

The regional-hospital route received the highest weighted score.

It failed the minimum-access condition for residents unable to reach the hospital.

The score could not compensate for the failure.

## Finding

Protected conditions must remain outside ordinary scalar tradeoff.

Their authority and override path must still remain reviewable.

---

# 4. Decision-method plurality

## Result

Pass.

The methods produced different leading routes:

```text
majority vote: renovation
weighted MCDA: regional hospital
outranking: combined local portfolio
robust trial: combined local portfolio
```

The process selected the robust trial because uncertainty and reversibility were material.

## Finding

The decision method changes what question the system answers.

It must remain visible in the witness.

---

# 5. Route portfolio

## Result

Pass.

The county selected:

- a mobile clinic;
- local-practice partnerships;
- engineering review of renovation.

The routes shared a minimum-access condition and public review.

Schedule conflict with shift workers remained visible.

## Finding

A portfolio can preserve plurality and learning.

It must not conceal coordination burden or push difficult navigation onto vulnerable participants.

---

# 6. No-decision and delay

## Result

Pass.

The county deferred the permanent facility decision.

The record identified patients, future patients, staff, and taxpayers as delay or uncertainty bearers.

Temporary urgent access remained active.

## Finding

No-decision is a route through the field.

It preserves the status quo and distributes cost.

---

# 7. Dissent

## Result

Pass.

Shift workers supported local service while opposing daytime-only mobile hours.

Their prediction of lower east-neighborhood utilization remained attached to the final portfolio.

## Finding

Dissent is public memory, not procedural residue.

It allows consequence to remain answerable to reasons the selected route did not absorb.

---

# 8. Consequence return

## Result

Pass.

At ninety days:

- urgent access remained available;
- east-neighborhood use reached only 58 percent of the county average;
- partner practices absorbed more demand than expected.

The shift-hours prediction was partly confirmed.

The county reopened navigation and added evening sessions.

## Finding

Implementation is not the end of public intelligence.

Consequence changes the field and may change the route.

# 10. Remaining implementation risks

## Standing capture

Conveners may admit representatives who are convenient rather than accountable.

Representation sources and correction must remain open.

## Option flooding

Unlimited model-generated options may exhaust participants and obscure practical routes.

Option generation needs scope and burden controls.

## Protected-condition inflation

Groups may relabel ordinary preferences as protected conditions.

Competent authority and review are required.

## Method shopping

Decision makers may select whichever method produces a preferred answer.

Method selection should precede final comparison where possible.

## Portfolio dumping

Complexity may be shifted to participants least able to navigate it.

Administrative burden must remain part of the cost map.

## Dissent weaponization

Dissent trails may be used for retaliation or permanent labeling.

Publication and privacy require scope.

## Consequence selection

Institutions may publish only outcomes that support the selected route.

Consequence return requires independent and participant correction.
