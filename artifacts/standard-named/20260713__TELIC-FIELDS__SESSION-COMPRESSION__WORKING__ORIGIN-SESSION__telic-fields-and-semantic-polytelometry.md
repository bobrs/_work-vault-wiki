# Telic Fields and Semantic Polytelometry

## Inline summary

The conversation began by asking whether attractors are fundamentally teleological. It developed into a wider relational architecture: centers of standing inhabit local telic fields; 🜁 dyads allow those fields to meet without requiring either to disappear; semantics carries compressed orientation between them; witness carries context through time; loops make relational orientation durable; and higher systems emerge through repeated composition across scale.

The same architecture appears internally. A person is not one voice but a constitutional federation of locally telic elements. Each element carries some orientation toward states it seeks, protects, fears, or avoids. The person-level self emerges through the relationships among those elements rather than from a single permanent executive center.

Because neither people nor systems can hold every relevant difference at once, they operate through simplified telic projections. Much of cognition and coordination consists of estimating what matters to other centers of standing, acting on those estimates, witnessing the result, and recalculating. Context carrying capacity limits how much telic difference a loop can keep active without suppressing standing or collapsing complexity into coercive simplification.

This led to a new implementation use for language models. An LLM can compare multiple partially expressed telic projections before they are reduced to metrics or objective functions. Its legitimate role is provisional cartographer, comparator, translator, and compression witness—not judge, oracle, diagnostician, or sovereign interpreter. Such a system requires an open, consentfully sourced, locally governable, inspectable, forkable model whose own telic position and authority remain visible.

The emerging general capability is **semantic polytelometry**: maintaining a corrigible representation of multiple expressed ends long enough for their owners to see, correct, and coordinate them without pretending those ends are one.

## 2. The movement of the conversation

### 2.1 Attractors and teleology

The opening question asked whether attractors are fundamentally teleological objects.

The working distinction became:

> **An attractor describes convergence. A telos describes the end-relative organization of correction.**

An attractor identifies where trajectories tend to go. A telos identifies a state, condition, completion, or future treated as success, preservation, or proper direction.

An attractor need not imply intention. A system becomes more recognizably teleological when it interprets deviation from an end as error and organizes correction around preserving or attaining that end.

Candidate compression:

> **An attractor is not a purpose. It is the shape that makes purpose causally durable.**

### 2.2 Declared and operative teloi

The example of Facebook clarified the distinction among layers of purpose.

A system may have:

- a **declared telos**: what it says it is for;
- an **encoded telos**: what its rules, incentives, comparators, and metrics specify;
- an **operative telos**: what it actually preserves and restores when priorities conflict;
- an **emergent telos**: an end-like pattern generated through interaction without explicit adoption;
- a **consented telos**: an end whose authority to organize participants' energy has been meaningfully accepted;
- a **protected telos**: a condition the system is required to preserve.

The diagnostic question became:

> **Which objective is allowed to sacrifice the others?**

A mission statement may express a genuine aspiration while the system's correction mechanisms remain disciplined by profit, engagement, growth, continuity, safety, or institutional survival.

Candidate term:

> **Teleological incoherence** occurs when declared, encoded, operative, and emergent teloi diverge materially while the system continues to represent them as one.

### 2.3 Polytelometric navigation

The conversation then named a broader field.

> **Polytelometry makes the multiple ends acting upon a system legible in relation to one another.**

> **Polytelometric navigation permits movement without pretending those ends are one.**

This differs from optimization. Optimization ordinarily assumes that the objective has already been selected or collapsed into a calculable function. Polytelometric navigation retains plurality long enough to examine:

- which ends are present;
- where they came from;
- whose standing authorizes them;
- which are instrumental to others;
- which ends conflict;
- whose energy sustains them;
- which ends are protected;
- what each route preserves or sacrifices;
- who may revise, refuse, fork, or leave.

A governing warning emerged:

> **No telos becomes sovereign merely because it can be measured, optimized, or made into an attractor.**

## 3. The teleological field and the semantic field

An early formulation treated the teleological field as terrain and the semantic field as the accumulated trace of intelligence navigating it.

The distinction was then refined toward a more local and compositional account.

### 3.1 Local origin

The primitive is not necessarily one universal field of purpose.

The more restrained starting point is:

> **A center of standing is something for whom some possible states matter differently from others.**

When one possible state matters differently from another for a center of standing, a minimal telic orientation exists.

A **local telic field** is the end-relative landscape generated by those differences.

It may contain:

- states sought or avoided;
- viability conditions;
- protected boundaries;
- latent or explicit goals;
- attractions and aversions;
- possible futures;
- costs and sacrifices;
- capacities for correction;
- current uncertainty.

The field is indexed to a center of standing. It should not yet be treated as a cosmic substance.

### 3.2 The dyadic encounter

Two centers of standing enter relation:

\[
A(T_A) \;\; 🜁 \;\; B(T_B)
\]

Each retains its own local telic field. The 🜁 relation does not collapse them into one objective.

The encounter produces a relational field containing possibilities that exist only because the relation exists:

- continuation;
- mutual benefit;
- misunderstanding;
- conflict;
- shared work;
- obligation;
- repair;
- exit;
- emergent ends.

Working claim:

> **Individual standing grounds local telic significance. The 🜁 dyad is the atomic unit through which sovereign telic fields enter shared reality.**

### 3.3 Semantics as navigational compression

Semantics emerges when differences that matter to one center must become legible to another center or downstream process.

A signal becomes meaningful because it changes anticipated movement, available action, risk, interpretation, or relation.

Candidate formulations:

> **Meaning is a navigational distinction preserved because it mattered to some center of standing.**

> **Semantics carries compressed orientation.**

> **The semantic field is the durable residue of prior telic navigation.**

Language, categories, contracts, rituals, interfaces, roles, metrics, and institutions are durable trails. Later navigators can mistake those trails for the terrain itself.

That produces failures such as:

- a metric becoming the mission;
- a legal category becoming more authoritative than the relationship it was meant to describe;
- a business model being treated as a law of nature;
- an inherited word carrying an obsolete teleological arrangement;
- a protocol representation becoming more authoritative than its source relation.

Candidate warning:

> **When the path is mistaken for the terrain, inherited meaning becomes destiny.**

## 4. Dependent loops and constitutional grounding

A major structural claim emerged:

> **No derived loop is self-grounding.**

Every higher relationship depends upon constitutive lower relationships that made it possible. A contract depends upon language, institutions, identity, recognition, and prior willingness to enter commitment. An organization depends upon human relationships, tacit knowledge, cultural interpretation, and a shared account of what the organization is for.

The higher loop compresses the lower loops. It does not abolish them.

### 4.1 Maintenance and recovery

A useful distinction:

> **Maintenance operates within an intact constitutional substrate. Recovery must recreate, replace, or release that substrate.**

Maintenance remains possible while parties still share enough language, evidence, purpose, and legitimacy to identify deviation and return.

When they no longer agree about what counts as evidence, authority, obligation, standing, or the relationship's purpose, the loop cannot repair itself solely through its existing rules.

Recovery may require:

- return to a base 🜁 exchange;
- context transplantation into another viable loop;
- constitutional re-foundation;
- release or clean dissolution.

### 4.2 Teleological graft rejection

Candidate definition:

> **Teleological graft rejection occurs when context transplanted from one loop cannot remain coherent inside another because the receiving loop organizes action around incompatible ends.**

The form may survive while the living meaning dies.

Examples include:

- acquisitions that preserve a product but destroy what made it valuable;
- institutions importing rituals without the commitments that sustained them;
- AI systems preserving an instruction while optimizing away its originating human purpose;
- relationships resuming routines without restoring their constitutive conditions.

Candidate phrase:

> **The form survives. The loop does not.**

## 5. The constitutional self

The conversation increased the fractal resolution of the model.

The person is not necessarily the smallest telic unit. A person may already be a federated or constitutional loop.

Internal Family Systems and many other practices recognize the ordinary experience of internal negotiation: protectors, critics, caretakers, planners, avoiders, injured child-states, bodily needs, moral commitments, professional identities, and other recurring organizations influence action.

The broader structural claim is:

> **A person-level telic field is generated through relationships among constitutional elements, each carrying a local field of concern, protection, attraction, and avoidance.**

### 5.1 Constitutional elements

A constitutional element is provisionally understood as a relatively persistent internal organization that:

- distinguishes preferred from threatening states;
- attempts to influence action;
- protects something it regards as important;
- carries memory, learned orientation, or embodied expectation;
- responds to other elements;
- can enter coordination or conflict.

It need not be a complete personality or legally independent agent.

Each element has local telic standing within the internal constitution even though the integrated person remains the externally sovereign participant.

### 5.2 Internal 🜁 dyads

The same base relation appears internally:

\[
e_A(T_A) \;\; 🜁 \;\; e_B(T_B)
\]

For example:

> The part that needs rest is here.

> The part that fears what will happen if we stop is also here.

The purpose is not immediate agreement. It is the preservation of standing long enough for conflict to become navigable rather than annihilative.

Working claim:

> **The 🜁 relationships among constitutional elements ground the sub-agent local telic fields from which person-level orientation emerges.**

### 5.3 Executive control is not selfhood

One internal element or coalition may gain executive control without representing the whole person.

This produced several distinctions:

- **local telic standing**: an element's concerns must be represented internally;
- **person-level sovereignty**: the integrated person enters external consent, obligation, and relationship;
- **executive authority**: the process currently controlling action;
- **constitutional legitimacy**: whether executive action adequately represents and protects the internal field.

Candidate statement:

> **Executive control is not the same thing as selfhood.**

### 5.4 Internal consent

Internal consent does not require unanimity. It requires adequate representation of affected elements, protection of their standing, visible dissent, revisability, and witnessed consequences.

A consentful internal action may be:

> Part of me does not want this.

> I understand what it is protecting.

> We are not erasing that concern.

> We are proceeding within boundaries that preserve its standing.

> We will witness what happens and revise if necessary.

Candidate compression:

> **The self is not a telos. It is a constitution through which many local teloi learn whether they can inhabit one life together.**

## 6. Internal telic fields and simplified telic field calculation

A major fork separated the lived field from the model used by downstream dependents.

### 6.1 Internal telic field

The **internal telic field** is the full, changing landscape of states that matter to a center of standing.

It includes more than conscious goals:

- desired outcomes;
- protected conditions;
- feared outcomes;
- bodily and emotional needs;
- identity commitments;
- obligations;
- unresolved conflicts;
- latent orientations;
- incompatible wishes;
- temporal commitments;
- things the being cannot yet articulate.

The field need not be coherent. Plurality is expected.

### 6.2 Simplified telic field calculation

A downstream participant cannot access the entire field. It must calculate a scoped approximation:

> What matters to this person, part, or system here, now, and within this relationship enough to guide the next action?

The process is the **simplified telic field calculation**.

The resulting representation is a **simplified telic projection**.

\[
\text{Internal telic field}
\xrightarrow[\text{scope, context, bandwidth}]{\text{compression}}
\text{Simplified telic projection}
\]

Much human cognition consists of repeatedly doing this:

\[
\text{estimate}
\rightarrow
\text{action}
\rightarrow
\text{response}
\rightarrow
\text{updated estimate}
\]

Management, caregiving, diplomacy, parenting, negotiation, therapy, law, design, sales, politics, and ordinary social life depend heavily on estimating what matters to other centers of standing.

### 6.3 Institutions as projection systems

Institutions create reusable projections:

- mission statements;
- roles;
- contracts;
- policies;
- prices;
- KPIs;
- service-level agreements;
- budgets;
- job descriptions;
- dashboards;
- escalation procedures;
- prompts.

Each tells downstream processes which outcomes matter, how strongly, and under what constraints.

A KPI is a simplified telic projection.

A contract is a simplified relational telic projection.

A prompt is a temporary telic projection given to an AI.

### 6.4 Projection failure

The downstream system acts on the projection, not the full field.

\[
\widehat{T} \neq T
\]

Failure can occur when:

- an essential concern was omitted;
- the field changed;
- the projection was false or strategic;
- the receiver interpreted it through another culture or ontology;
- the receiver optimized the projection so aggressively that it destroyed the field it represented.

Three breach locations were distinguished:

1. **Field breach** — action violates something genuinely protected in the originating field.
2. **Projection breach** — the downstream actor violates the representation it received.
3. **Projection-integrity breach** — the representation was inadequate, stale, misleading, unauthorized, or incompatible with the field it claimed to express.

Candidate term:

> **Telic projection integrity** is the degree to which a scoped projection remains adequate for the relationship and action it is governing.

## 7. Context carrying capacity

A further fork concerned the limit on active relational complexity.

> **No element, person, or system can hold the full telic field of everything it depends upon.**

This is not merely a memory limit.

### 7.1 Definition

> **Context carrying capacity is the amount and complexity of relevant telic difference a loop can hold without suppressing standing, losing coherence, or collapsing into coercive simplification.**

A person may remember many facts while being unable to hold two conflicting needs in active relation.

An institution may possess every record while allowing only a quarterly metric into executive context.

An AI may have access to an archive while acting from a narrow prompt and selected retrieval.

### 7.2 Types of omission

Not all omission is breach.

- **Protective omission** — context is temporarily withheld because the receiver cannot safely metabolize it.
- **Capacity omission** — context is absent because the loop cannot presently hold it.
- **Strategic compression** — only the information necessary for the scoped decision is transmitted.
- **Distortive omission** — removed context changes the apparent meaning or telos.
- **Constitutional erasure** — a relevant element is systematically denied representation, so its standing cannot affect decisions.

The sensitive rule is that increasing context is not automatically beneficial. Partition, suppression, or compartmentalization may have protected the system.

Candidate principle:

> **Increase the capacity for consentful context exchange without exceeding the ability of participating elements to remain present.**

Candidate practical statement:

> **A healthy self can route the right context to the right relationship at the right resolution.**

### 7.3 Capacity overflow

When a loop exceeds capacity, it must:

- compress visibly;
- delegate;
- sequence;
- partition;
- slow down;
- recruit an outer loop;
- or stop.

Otherwise it silently drops standing.

This connects to an existing governance invariant:

> When a loop can no longer preserve protected human stakes, it must halt, degrade, or reroute.

## 8. Temporal telic relations

The architecture then stitched across time.

A relationship may exist between:

- present and past selves;
- present and future selves;
- one constitutional element and the temporal terrain it still inhabits;
- founders and successors;
- promise-makers and later inheritors;
- institutions and later states of their own mission.

At minimum:

\[
A_t \;\; 🜁 \;\; A_{t+1}
\]

Identity may be understood as a continuity protocol that carries selected context across changing states.

### 8.1 Temporal standing

Candidate definition:

> **Temporal standing is the claim that later states of a continuing being must remain represented in present decisions that materially constrain them.**

The present executive temporarily governs consequences another version of the self will inherit.

The future self does not possess a direct current voice, but its stakes are not therefore zero.

### 8.2 Different temporal terrains inside the self

A protector may still act from a world that existed decades ago. The adult system may know the current terrain while the protector knows only the earlier danger.

Internal repair may therefore require temporal context exchange:

- what happened then;
- what remains true;
- what changed;
- what protection is still needed;
- what old strategy now creates new harm.

### 8.3 Mirrors

Agents usually act toward internal models rather than directly toward full fields.

Inside \(B\), there exists a mirror or estimate of \(A\):

\[
M_{A \mid B}(t)
\]

A person may act toward:

- a remembered parent;
- an anticipated boss;
- an imagined audience;
- a modeled future self;
- an institution as internally represented;
- a partner as interpreted.

The actual source may be absent while the mirror continues governing action.

### 8.4 Witness as temporal stitching

> **Witness is the continuity mechanism by which standing and telic context survive beyond the moment in which they were directly present.**

Witness prevents each new executive state from silently redefining the past.

It carries:

- prior purpose;
- prior consent;
- prior uncertainty;
- promises;
- consequences;
- revisions;
- unresolved breach.

Candidate compression:

> **Standing generates telic difference. Dyads generate relation. Semantics carries orientation. Witness carries context through time. Loops compose the result across scale.**

## 10. Semantic polytelometry with language models

The conversation identified a new use class for LLMs.

> **LLMs can compare expressed significance before significance is reduced to metrics.**

Given several telic projections, a model may help identify:

- shared ends;
- compatible ends;
- conditionally compatible ends;
- direct conflicts;
- asymmetric dependencies;
- differently defined terms;
- hidden instrumental relationships;
- protected boundaries;
- missing context;
- uncertain interpretations;
- candidate routes and their sacrifices.

### 10.1 Example operation

One participant says:

> I need the project finished quickly because the team is losing confidence.

Another says:

> I need more time because releasing now will create problems we cannot repair later.

A shallow reading sees speed versus delay.

A telic comparison may see:

- protection of team confidence;
- protection of system reliability;
- shared concern for project survival;
- differing estimates of which failure is more dangerous;
- differing definitions of "finished";
- a possible staged route preserving more of both fields.

This is semantic intersection under differing descriptions of possible futures.

### 10.2 Proper role

The model may say:

> Based on the present statements, these appear to be the relevant projections.

It may not say:

> This is what you truly want.

Legitimate roles:

- provisional cartographer;
- comparator;
- translator;
- ambiguity detector;
- compression witness;
- provenance keeper;
- candidate-route generator.

Illegitimate roles:

- sovereign interpreter;
- owner of the telic map;
- judge of the participants' true ends;
- unreviewable optimizer;
- substitute source of consent;
- authority that erases unresolved remainder.

### 10.3 A possible comparison record

A semantic polytelometric output may include:

- projection A;
- projection B;
- shared field;
- compatible field;
- conflict field;
- unresolved field;
- semantic ambiguity;
- missing participants or standing;
- candidate routes;
- what each route preserves;
- what each route sacrifices;
- who bears each cost;
- required consent;
- witness requirements;
- recalculation triggers;
- confidence and uncertainty.

The output is not a verdict. It is a corrigible map.

### 10.4 Internal application

An LLM may also compare projections among constitutional elements:

> Part of me wants to leave.

> Part of me is terrified of being alone.

> Part of me is exhausted.

> Part of me believes leaving would make me bad.

A useful reflection may distinguish vitality, attachment, exhaustion, and inherited moral rules without declaring which part is the "true self."

This increases internal context resolution while preserving person-level sovereignty.

## 11. Consentfully trained models

The capability cannot be implemented coherently with openness defined only as downloadable weights.

A stronger requirement emerged:

> **An open, consentfully sourced, locally governable, inspectable, forkable model whose own telic position is declared and whose outputs never outrank the standing of the participants.**

Minimum requirements include:

### 11.1 Consentful provenance

Training data provenance should remain legible:

- what was used;
- under what authority;
- for what purposes;
- with what exclusions or downstream conditions;
- with what withdrawal or non-participation mechanisms where feasible.

### 11.2 Inspectable operation

The system should expose:

- context received;
- inferences made;
- compressions performed;
- omissions;
- uncertainty;
- changes to the working telic map.

This does not require solved mechanistic interpretability. It requires procedural legibility adequate to contest the calculation.

### 11.3 Non-sovereign output

Inferred projections remain provisional, attributable, correctable, and consent-bound.

### 11.4 Sovereign execution

Sensitive uses may require:

- local inference;
- participant-controlled context;
- scoped retention;
- no secondary training by default;
- private semantic runtimes;
- participant-controlled records and deletion policies.

### 11.5 Plural and declared alignment

The model cannot pretend to be neutral. Its alignment, safety rules, institutional constraints, and commercial objectives are additional teloi in the field.

Those must be declared and governed rather than hidden.

### 11.6 Forkability

Forkability is not only a software freedom. It is a constitutional safeguard against model, ontology, or institutional drift.

Candidate invariant:

> **A system cannot consentfully map telic fields while its own field remains hidden, unaccountable, and non-consensual.**

Stronger bridge:

> **Consentful provenance in. Consentful interpretation out.**
