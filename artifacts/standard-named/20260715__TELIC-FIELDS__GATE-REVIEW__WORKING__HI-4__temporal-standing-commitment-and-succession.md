# HI-4 Gate Review

Status: pass with conditions  
Content canon status: unset

## Gate question

Can the Telic Field architecture preserve commitments, consent, future standing, option closure, succession, and repair across time without:

- inventing future consent;
- making the past sovereign;
- allowing stale authority to persist;
- erasing urgent present standing;
- treating every commitment as permanent;
- permitting successors to inherit assets without obligations;
- treating release as historical erasure;
- treating repair as forced continuation?

## Overall result

# PASS WITH CONDITIONS

I.4 and H.4 may advance.

No frozen term requires retirement.

Temporal standing remains viable as a governance category distinct from legal standing and fictional future consent.

---

# 1. Reversible present action

## Result

Pass.

The present selected an eight-week training trial rather than a permanent commitment.

The route preserved:

- continued participation;
- pause;
- return to the prior schedule;
- review after evidence.

Only a small enrollment fee became irreversible.

## Finding

Future-standing review does not require preserving every option.

It requires visibility into which options close, who inherits closure, and whether a more reversible route can produce the needed learning.

---

# 2. Valid commitment

## Result

Pass.

The study commitment governed Tuesday evening because:

- formation authority was valid;
- scope was explicit;
- the valid interval was active;
- no exception applied;
- review and release remained available.

## Finding

A commitment can legitimately bind future action.

Its legitimacy comes from bounded authority and continuing review, not from permanence.

# 3. Changed circumstances

## Result

Pass.

A material caregiving change moved the commitment to:

```text
DUE_FOR_REVIEW
```

The review narrowed the schedule and preserved:

Revision does not prove the original commitment was invalid.

It proves the commitment was governed rather than worshipped.

# 4. Stale projection

## Result

Pass.

The old automatic-renewal preference remained historically visible.

A later direct statement made it stale.

The old projection could not authorize a new renewal.

## Finding

A system must distinguish:

```text
accurate history
≠ current authority
```

Persistence is not renewal.

# 5. Expired consent

## Result

Pass.

January sharing remained historically authorized under the January consent.

A July model-training use failed.

The recording's continued existence did not create a new permission.

## Finding

Consent must be versioned by purpose, recipient, operation, and time.

Historical validity and current activity are different states.

# 6. Future standing and irreversibility

## Result

Pass.

A permanent community-land transfer closed:

- public access;
- later governance;
- restoration routes.

Future standing was materially incomplete.

The action paused.

## Finding

Irreversibility strengthens the need for future-standing representation.

It does not create future consent or automatic future veto.

---

# 7. Succession

## Result

Pass.

The successor foundation received:

- a restricted housing fund;
- bounded administrative capability;
- open commitments;
- donor restrictions;
- an unresolved complaint;
- restricted records.

It did not automatically receive authority to:

- repurpose applicant data;
- change the beneficiary class;
- cancel commitments without review.

## Finding

Assets, capabilities, records, authority, and obligations must be separate transfer classes.

A successor may inherit the duty to answer without inheriting every predecessor power.

---

# 8. Temporal breach and repair

## Result

Pass with residual harm.

A stale projection caused an unauthorized automatic renewal.

Repair included:

## Finding

Temporal repair must state what can be restored and what cannot.

Repair may end the loop rather than preserve it.

# 10. Remaining implementation risks

## Fabricated future interests

Present actors may project convenient preferences onto later centers.

Future-standing sources and uncertainty must remain visible.

## Commitment asymmetry

Powerful parties may define reliance and release terms that create practical captivity.

Formation authority and exit burden require review.

## Emergency persistence

Temporary authority may continue after emergency conditions end.

Expiry must be enforced, not merely documented.

## Option-preservation inflation

Systems may preserve too many options and avoid legitimate commitment.

Materiality and action purpose must constrain review.

## Succession extraction

A successor may claim continuity selectively for assets, reputation, or market access.

Attached obligations and unresolved harms must remain visible.

## Bitemporal burden

Complete temporal history can become administrative surveillance.

Witness views require minimization and scoped access.
