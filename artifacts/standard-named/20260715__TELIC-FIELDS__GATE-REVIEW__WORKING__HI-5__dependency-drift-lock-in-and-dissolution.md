# HI-5 Gate Review

Status: pass with conditions  
Content canon status: unset

## Gate question

Can the Telic Field architecture make dependency governable without:

- pathologizing dependence itself;
- treating continued support as consent;
- hiding the burden required to maintain the loop;
- assuming copied form carries its supporting field;
- allowing mission or authority drift to remain invisible;
- describing formal exit as effective exit;
- ignoring withdrawal;
- preserving the loop at the expense of contributors;
- dissolving the form while abandoning what remains owed?

## Overall result

# PASS WITH CONDITIONS

I.5 and H.5 may advance.

No frozen term requires retirement.

`Dependent loop`, `supporting loop`, `telic incompatibility`, `constitutional substrate mismatch`, `capture`, `fork`, and `dissolution` remain viable candidate governance terms.

“Teleological graft rejection” remains acceptable as lineage language or explanatory metaphor only.

---

# 1. Legitimate dependency

## Result

Pass.

The community-meal loop depends on:

- volunteer labor and care;
- restricted funding;
- community legitimacy and governance.

Each contribution carries:

- authority;
- scope;
- burden limit;
- reciprocity;
- review;
- practical exit.

## Finding

The specification does not make independence the goal.

It distinguishes supported coordination from unauthorized recruitment.

---

# 2. Hidden recruitment

## Result

Pass.

Conversation content authorized for current assistance was recruited for persistent model training without an active authority basis.

The system:

- paused new recruitment;
- quarantined affected records;
- corrected the authority record;
- opened deletion or lawful-retention review;
- preserved downstream gaps.

## Finding

Repeated availability is not repeated permission.

A loop does not acquire support authority through habit.

---

# 3. Constitutional substrate mismatch

## Result

Pass.

The regional organization imported the visible software and scoring process used by a national organization.

It did not import:

- appeal capacity;
- offline access;
- accommodation review;
- legal review;
- maintenance funding.

The transplant failed the compatibility gate.

## Finding

A process is not portable merely because its interface is.

The supporting field is part of the architecture.

---

# 4. Mission drift

## Result

Pass.

The mutual-aid program's operative purpose shifted from meeting urgent needs toward maximizing reportable cases and grant renewal.

The old community authority had not authorized the material shift.

Expansion paused pending correction, reauthorization, fork, or release.

## Finding

Mission drift may be legitimate adaptation.

It becomes constitutionally material when beneficiaries, burdens, authority, or protected conditions change without review.

---

# 5. Support burden

## Result

Pass.

Volunteer maintainers were expected to contribute eight hours per week without on-call duty.

Observed burden became forty-two hours per week with continuous incident response.

The service still worked.

The burden was nevertheless disproportionate.

New enrollment paused.

## Finding

Output success does not establish support legitimacy.

A loop can function by consuming the standing and future possibility of its maintainers.

---

# 6. Lock-in and capture

## Result

Pass.

The user formally withdrew data authority.

Downstream retrieval and external copies continued using the profile.

The system recorded:

```text
formal exit: yes
effective exit: no
authority withdrawn: yes
support continues: yes
status: CAPTURED
```

The recruitment credential was disabled.

## Finding

Exit must alter the operating system.

Textual freedom without effective release is not enough.

---

# 7. Fork

## Result

Pass.

The privacy-first and open-data teams preserved:

- shared lineage;
- completed work;
- attribution;
- allocated assets;
- historical corrections.

They separated:

- future governance;
- future records;
- data-use authority;
- support recruitment;
- public identity.

## Finding

A fork can preserve relation without forcing merger.

Shared history does not require shared future sovereignty.

---

# 8. Dissolution

## Result

Pass.

The identity cooperative could no longer operate without captured volunteer maintenance or unauthorized vendor data use.

Dissolution:

- stopped new enrollment;
- maintained bounded emergency access;
- exported participant records;
- assigned restricted-data custody;
- allocated repair funds;
- preserved open complaints;
- scheduled deletion;
- issued public notice;
- ended future support recruitment.

## Finding

The service form ended.

Participant control, repair, history, and residual obligation remained.

Dissolution preserved more of the purpose than continued operation would have.

# 10. Remaining implementation risks

## Hidden-support discovery

The loop may not know every contribution that keeps it alive.

Dependency witnesses require challenge and correction.

## Burden quantification

Some care, legitimacy, emotional labor, and opportunity costs resist stable measurement.

The framework must preserve qualitative evidence.

## Strategic withdrawal

A critical contributor may use withdrawal to capture the dependent loop.

Support rights and residual duties need domain-specific review.

## False drift claims

Leaders may call necessary adaptation “mission drift” to preserve old authority.

Drift review must examine operative evidence rather than labels.

## Exit externalities

A departing contributor may shift severe cost to others.

Practical exit and residual obligation must be considered together.

## Dissolution capture

Incumbents may use dissolution urgency to transfer assets, data, or power improperly.

Independent residual-state review is required.
