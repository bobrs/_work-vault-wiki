# Telic Projection

## Working read

A telic projection is a scoped, lossy, provisional representation of what matters within a telic field, made available to another center or downstream process.

No receiver obtains the whole field.

A person expresses a need. A contract encodes a relationship. A policy compresses institutional purpose. A prompt instructs an AI. A feeling carries a report from an internal constitutional element. A metric represents one aspect of what a system is trying to preserve.

Each is a projection.

The projection is not the field itself.

\[
\widehat{T} \neq T
\]

## Core claim

> **The projection is what downstream action can see; the field is what still bears the consequences.**

## Why projections exist

Every relationship operates under limited context capacity.

A center cannot disclose every relevant state, history, fear, boundary, and possible future. A receiver could not hold all of it even if it were disclosed.

So the source or receiver compresses.

That compression may be:

- intentional;
- unconscious;
- institutional;
- cultural;
- algorithmic;
- embodied;
- strategic;
- protective;
- distorted;
- stale.

## Simplified telic field calculation

The process of producing or inferring a usable projection is the **simplified telic field calculation**.

It asks:

> What matters to this center here, now, within this scope, enough to guide the next action?

Possible inputs include:

- direct statements;
- prior behavior;
- tone and expression;
- role;
- authority;
- boundaries;
- incentives;
- witnessed history;
- cultural context;
- prior consequences;
- uncertainty.

The output is a simplified telic projection.

## Projection and mirror

A projection is what leaves or is inferred from the source.

A mirror is the model maintained inside the receiver.

\[
T_A
\rightarrow
\widehat{T}_{A \rightarrow B}
\rightarrow
M_{A \mid B}
\]

The receiver's mirror may diverge because of:

- prior expectations;
- memory;
- fear;
- cultural difference;
- ontology;
- incentives;
- selective attention;
- missing history;
- power.

A good projection can produce a poor mirror. A poor projection can occasionally produce a useful mirror. Neither should be assumed correct without witness and correction.

## Projection integrity

**Telic projection integrity** is the continued adequacy of a projection for the scope, time, relationship, and action it governs.

A projection should ideally carry:

- source;
- scope;
- time;
- confidence;
- protected conditions;
- authority;
- uncertainty;
- omitted context;
- revision state;
- expiration or recalculation triggers.

## Three breach locations

### Field breach

An action violates something genuinely protected within the source field.

### Projection breach

A downstream actor violates the projection it was given.

### Projection-integrity breach

The representation itself was inadequate, stale, misleading, unauthorized, distorted, or incompatible with the field it claimed to express.

Candidate formulation:

> **A system can comply with the projection and still betray the field.**

## Examples

### Prompt

A prompt is a temporary telic projection given to an AI.

The AI may satisfy the literal prompt while violating unstated but relevant interests.

### KPI

A KPI is a simplified projection of what an organization claims matters.

The organization may optimize the KPI until it damages the wider field.

### Feeling

A feeling may be a compressed report from an internal constitutional element.

The feeling is not the whole element. The element is not the whole person.

### Contract

A contract is a scoped relational projection.

It does not contain the whole cultural, moral, emotional, or institutional substrate that gives the agreement meaning.

## Consent

Consent does not require total access to another field.

It requires sufficient mutual projection for the scope of the relationship.

Candidate formulation:

> **Consent is not complete field disclosure. It is adequate mutual projection for the scope of the loop being formed.**

## Witness

Witness keeps the gap between field and projection visible.

It may record:

- what was expressed;
- what was inferred;
- what was omitted;
- how the receiver understood it;
- what action followed;
- what consequences occurred;
- how the source later corrected the map.

Candidate formulation:

> **Healthy loops do not eliminate the gap between field and projection. They keep it witnessable and correctable.**

## Risks of misuse

- treating an inferred projection as diagnosis;
- declaring what a source “really wants”;
- hiding model uncertainty;
- optimizing a projection as though it were the whole field;
- retaining a stale projection after the field changes;
- excluding less verbal centers from representation;
- treating a summary as more authoritative than its source.

## Open questions

- How should projection loss be estimated?
- How should embodied or nonverbal fields be represented?
- What makes a projection adequate for consent?
- When should a projection expire?
- How should competing projections from one source be handled?
- What minimum metadata is required for machine use?
