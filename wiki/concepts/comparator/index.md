# Comparator

Working definition: the disclosed evaluative standard by which an attention pattern, artifact, action, or outcome is judged.

A comparator names what counts as better, worse, faithful, distorted, loving, harmful, safe, costly, or successful inside a particular frame. It is not the same as a metric: a metric records a signal, while a comparator states how that signal is being interpreted and what it is being used to decide.

## Core claim

> **No attention claim is grounded until its comparator is named.**

The comparator is the contract that makes evaluation contestable without pretending that “the field” or “reality” is a self-evident authority. A participant may accept, reject, revise, or fork a comparator; the ability to do so is part of autonomy and consent.

## Hidden comparators

A hidden comparator makes a situated judgment feel like fact. It can turn vigilance into care, intervention into protection, compliance into safety, or optimization into success without disclosing whose expectations, risks, time horizon, or protected conditions define the result.

The [Attention Engine](../../external/shimmerymemory/essays/attention-engine-book-series-plan-and-semantic-compression/index.md) calls the resulting diagnostic wrong-side error: the system spends effort repairing the visible symptom while the disagreement about frame or standard remains unnamed.

## Comparator and expectation set

An expectation set is the implied or explicit set of expectations under which a comparator operates: what should happen, what response is owed, which risks matter, what counts as adequate care, and what would count as repair. The comparator and expectation set should be disclosed together, especially when an action recruits another center’s energy or future possibility.
