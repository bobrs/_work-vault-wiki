# Consent to Context

Working definition: permission to enter the frame within which meaning, update, crossing, or evaluation will occur.

Consent to context is narrower than generic agreement and broader than a single permission click. It concerns the operative scope, stakes, timing, authority, expectations, and boundary conditions that make a semantic crossing intelligible.

## Core claim

> **A crossing is unstable when participants have not consented to the context that gives the crossing its meaning.**

Consent to context does not require complete disclosure or permanent agreement. It requires enough relevant context for a participant to know what game is being played, what may be recruited, what authority is being exercised, what can be revised, and how refusal, pause, repair, or release remain available.

## Relation to wrong-side error

When consent to context is missing, attention often repairs the wrong side of the error. Participants argue over wording, tone, or evidence while the real breach is that they never shared the frame under which those signals were supposed to count.

This is why comparator and expectation-set disclosure matter. A person can consent to an action in one frame and reject the same action in another without contradiction.

## Relation to telic fields

Consent determines whether one center’s telos may legitimately recruit another center’s attention, energy, data, labor, standing, or future possibility. Context must therefore carry enough information about protected conditions and consequences for consent to remain meaningful without demanding total exposure.
