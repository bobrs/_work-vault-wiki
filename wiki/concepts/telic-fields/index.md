# Telic Fields

Parent lineage: [The Telic Field Papers](../../projects/telic-field-papers/index.md)

Status: candidate  
Source role: semantic wiki layer  
Processing tier: 4  
Content canon status: unset

## Working read

A telic field is the end-relative landscape generated by the differences that matter to a center of standing.

The concept begins from a simple observation: possible futures are not equivalent for a living being, person, constitutional element, relationship, or institution. Some states are approached. Some are avoided. Some are protected. Some are feared. Some are treated as conditions of continuity, dignity, repair, or survival.

The field is not merely a list of goals. It may include embodied needs, latent orientations, boundaries, temporal commitments, inherited fears, explicit plans, operative incentives, protected states, and unresolved conflict.

The term is currently a working concept. It should not yet be treated as a newly discovered physical field, a complete formal theory, or an established technical term with one accepted meaning.

## Core claim

> **A telic field begins where possible futures cease to be equivalent for a center of standing.**

## Center of standing

A telic field is always indexed to something.

That center may be:

- a person;
- a constitutional element within a person;
- another living organism;
- a dyad or relationship;
- a group or institution;
- a future state of a continuing person;
- potentially an artificial or computational agent, subject to unresolved criteria.

Standing does not automatically imply consciousness, legal personhood, sovereignty, or equal authority in every context. It means that the center's consequences, protected conditions, or future states cannot be treated as structurally interchangeable.

## What a telic field may contain

A telic field may include:

- desired states;
- avoided states;
- viability conditions;
- protected boundaries;
- fears;
- attractions;
- obligations;
- identity commitments;
- active goals;
- latent ends;
- time horizons;
- acceptable and unacceptable costs;
- possible routes;
- expectations of correction;
- uncertainty;
- states not yet available to language.

## Local, relational, and higher-order fields

### Local telic field

The end-relative landscape of one center of standing.

### Relational telic field

The possibilities, obligations, threats, meanings, and emergent ends created because two or more centers are now in relation.

### Higher-order telic field

The field of a larger loop, group, organization, or institution that emerges from lower relations but remains dependent upon them.

The higher field is not automatically sovereign over the lower fields that constitute it.

## Relation to attractors

An attractor describes where trajectories tend to converge.

A telos identifies an end, preservation condition, completion, or future relative to which action is organized.

A system may have:

- a declared telos without an attractor capable of realizing it;
- a strong attractor without consciously adopting the corresponding telos;
- several teloi operating at different scales;
- an operative attractor that contradicts its declared purpose.

Candidate formulation:

> **An attractor is not a purpose. It is the shape that makes purpose causally durable.**

## Relation to semantics

A telic field becomes semantically relevant when distinctions that matter to one center must become legible to another center or downstream process.

A signal carries meaning because it changes anticipated movement, available action, risk, relation, or interpretation.

Candidate formulation:

> **Semantics carries compressed orientation.**

## Relation to consent

Consent determines whether one center's telos may legitimately recruit another center's energy, attention, data, standing, labor, resources, or future possibility.

Candidate formulation:

> **Consent determines which purposes may legitimately recruit which energies.**

## Relation to loops

A loop makes an orientation durable by allowing it to return through memory, correction, rhythm, exchange, or renewed relation.

A living loop does not merely repeat its form. It must retain some path back to the constitutive standing and relations that made it meaningful.

## Risks of misuse

This concept can be misused if it becomes:

- a substitute for listening;
- a claim to know another person's true purpose;
- a scalar utility function imposed on plural ends;
- a justification for treating every life as a project;
- a metaphysical claim stronger than the available evidence;
- a way to erase rest, purposelessness, refusal, or release.

A telic field does not require one grand purpose.

Plural, conflicting, dormant, and revisable ends are expected.

## Open questions

- Is a telic field best modeled as a state space, graph, geometry, control surface, phenomenological field, or disciplined metaphor?
- What minimum properties establish a center of standing?
- Can nonliving systems possess telic fields, or only encoded objective structures?
- How do fields compose without collapsing into one utility function?
- How should protected conditions differ from teloi?
- How does a telic field change through learning, trauma, culture, embodiment, and time?
