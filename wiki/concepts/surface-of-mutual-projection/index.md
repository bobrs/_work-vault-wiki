# Surface of Mutual Projection

Working definition: the relational semantic surface formed by intention, reception, projection, memory, expectation, comparator, and response.

Communication does not simply travel across this surface as a message passed between isolated minds. Meaning is formed, revised, and returned on the surface itself. In an unwitnessed dyad, the inside and outside of the exchange can become Möbius-shaped: what one participant thinks they said returns as the other participant’s interpretation of what was meant.

## Core claim

> **Meaning lives on the surface of mutual projection, where each response changes the field to which the next response is addressed.**

This is not a claim that every interpretation is equally valid. The surface becomes more legible when artifacts, comparators, expectation sets, consent to context, and witness are available. Those supports make disagreement traceable without turning one participant into the sovereign owner of the field.

## Relation to artifact and attractor

A sentence, transcript, recording, or timestamp can be a semantic artifact. The meaning organized around it is an attractor-like return pattern rather than a second artifact hidden inside the words. Confusing artifact and attractor causes participants to argue over the object while missing the relation that gives it force.
