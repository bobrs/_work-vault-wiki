# Contextual Authority Principle

Working definition: authority that is local, temporary, skill-indexed, and revocable, so coordination can reduce uncertainty without hardening into permanent dominance.

The incoming draft makes a useful governance claim: when systems scale, authority works best when it is scoped to the situation that currently needs judgment. That keeps power legible as a function of context rather than identity. It also gives the wiki a clean bridge between governance, witness, and consent, because each action can be checked against the context that authorized it.

In Work Vault terms, CAP helps explain why governance pages keep returning to revocation, legitimacy, and answerability. It is a compact way to say that authority should move to the agent best able to reduce uncertainty in the moment, then move back when the moment changes.
